Reading Results and Comparing Models

Unreleased documentation preview — 13 October 2026

This manual includes unreleased interfaces and examples. It is not the documentation of an installed release. Model support and scientific acceptance remain as stated on each page.

See the Visualization Gallery for Wright Maps, Pathway Maps and MGMFRM response surfaces with worked reading guidance.

A completed fit produces numbers, but an analysis needs an answer supported by those numbers. Read the output in this order: computational diagnostics, uncertainty for the quantity of interest, predictive adequacy, and sensitivity to defensible assumptions. Model comparison then addresses a specified question.

Separate three kinds of uncertainty

QuantityWhat it describesWhat it does not establish
Posterior intervalUncertainty about a parameter or contrast conditional on the model, prior and observed data.Repeated-study coverage at every possible fixed truth.
Monte Carlo standard error, or MCSENumerical uncertainty in an estimated posterior summary due to a finite MCMC run.Small measurement uncertainty or a correct model.
Simulation replication standard errorUncertainty in an average performance measure across independently generated datasets.Precision obtained by treating many parameters or correlated draws as independent datasets.

For example, a wide ability interval with a small MCSE means that its posterior summary is computed precisely but the ability remains uncertain. More adequate MCMC draws can reduce numerical error; they do not replace more informative ratings. A narrow interval also needs diagnostic review and checks of the model and prior before substantive use.

R-hat compares behavior across chains, effective sample size (ESS) describes information in dependent draws, and divergences flag problematic HMC behavior. Inspect bulk and tail ESS, because interval endpoints can be much less precise than a posterior mean. Passing diagnostic thresholds is useful computation evidence, not a proof of convergence, identification, recovery or substantive validity. The fitting guide explains the available diagnostic output.

Choose 90%, 95% or 99% credible intervals

A credible interval (CrI) needs both a probability mass and an interval rule. The current summaries and plots use central, equal-tailed intervals (ETIs): a 90% ETI runs from the 5th to the 95th posterior percentile, while a 99% ETI runs from the 0.5th to the 99.5th percentile. Neither 90% nor 95% is a package-wide requirement; defaults differ across functions. Choose a level appropriate to the reporting question and state it explicitly.

For an existing scalar MFRM fit, set both the main columns and interval list:

using BayesianMGMFRM

rows99 = posterior_summary(fit;
    lower = 0.005, upper = 0.995, intervals = (0.99,))

# The top-level columns and this nested entry now both describe 99% ETIs.
first(rows99).lower
first(rows99).upper
only(first(rows99).intervals)

# Several levels can be retained together; main columns still use lower/upper.
rows_all = posterior_summary(fit;
    lower = 0.005, upper = 0.995, intervals = (0.90, 0.95, 0.99))

lower and upper are quantile probabilities; intervals contains interval masses. Supplying only intervals = (0.99,) adds the requested nested interval but leaves the main lower/upper columns at their default 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. There is no interval = 0.99 keyword for this summary function.

For experimental GMFRM and MGMFRM, posterior_summary describes raw computational coordinates. Use the qualified model-scale summary for positive loadings and rater consistencies:

model_rows99 = BayesianMGMFRM.direct_posterior_summary(fit;
    lower = 0.005, upper = 0.995, intervals = (0.99,))

Fixed-coefficient multidimensional MFRM also supports these two summary calls; its direct summary reconstructs model coordinates. Always report the coordinate and units of the quantity being summarized.

The plotting and response-surface interfaces instead take a single mass:

OutputExplicit 99% requestScope
Parameter interval plotBayesianMGMFRM.plot_posterior(fit; interval = 0.99)Supported saved-fit types; model coordinates by default.
Wright MapBayesianMGMFRM.plot_wright(fit; interval = 0.99)Stable scalar MFRM.

Load CairoMakie for the plotting calls; the numerical surface needs no renderer. Titles and figure notes remain outside images by default. In a Wright histogram, person bars count posterior medians; they are not individual credible intervals. The interval argument applies to the interval summaries, not to histogram bins. Surface intervals concern each conditional probability or expected rating at one ability grid point. They are not simultaneous bands or predictive intervals for individual future scores.

Changing the interval level reuses the existing draws and does not require refitting. Means and medians remain unchanged; 99% ETIs are at least as wide as 90% ETIs computed from the same draws. However, farther-tail endpoints need adequate numerical precision. Request MCSE at the endpoints actually reported:

precision99 = posterior_mcse(fit; probabilities = (0.005, 0.5, 0.995))

The default coordinate for this MCSE call matches the identified scalar MFRM or the direct generalized/multidimensional model coordinates. To match a raw GMFRM/MGMFRM summary instead, add parameter_space = :raw_unconstrained. Inspect the returned status and endpoint MCSE together with chain diagnostics; a returned number alone does not establish tail precision. The Stan posterior documentation explains quantile MCSE. Existing 90% simulation coverage results do not become 99% coverage evidence simply by changing an output setting.

Is a highest-density interval available?

BayesianMGMFRM currently has no integrated HDI/HPD summary or plot option. An ETI is already a Bayesian credible interval; it is not an approximation to an HDI. For a continuous unimodal posterior, an HDI selects the shortest interval containing the requested mass. Unlike an ETI, its excluded tail probabilities need not be equal. The two can differ substantially for skewed posteriors.

For example, a unit-rate exponential posterior has a 90% ETI approximately $[0.0513,2.9957]$ and a 90% HDI $[0,2.3026]$. Both contain 90% probability; they answer different interval-selection questions. The density is highest at zero, explaining why the HDI starts there. With separated modes, a highest-density region can have disconnected pieces; forcing one interval can span low-density values. HDIs also depend on parameterization: computing an HDI for a log loading and exponentiating its endpoints need not give the HDI of the positive loading. Choose the scientific reporting scale first.

The separate Julia package PosteriorStats.jl provides hdi(values; prob = 0.99) and explicitly documented multimodal methods. Here values must contain draws of one chosen scalar parameter or draw-wise contrast on the intended scale, not a matrix pooling different parameters. This is an optional external calculation, not a BayesianMGMFRM API or dependency; it is not installed or exercised by this guide. Retain the original chain ordering for diagnostics, label the resulting interval as an HDI, and assess its numerical stability. The ETI endpoint MCSE call above does not by itself supply MCSE for estimated HDI boundaries. Changing interval rules cannot repair a poorly explored posterior.

Interpret a contrast, not overlapping marginal intervals

Suppose the question is whether P1 has higher communication ability than P2. For each joint posterior draw $s$, calculate

\[\Delta^{(s)}=\theta_{\mathrm{P1,communication}}^{(s)} -\theta_{\mathrm{P2,communication}}^{(s)}.\]

Summarize these differences directly. Do not subtract the endpoints of two marginal intervals: the two abilities can be dependent in the posterior. A hypothetical 90% posterior interval $[0.10,0.70]$ supports a positive difference under the fitted assumptions, but its practical importance requires a justified ability unit and a substantive benchmark. An interval spanning zero does not by itself establish equivalence.

For raters, a positive severity contrast means the first rater is more severe under the model's sign convention. A consistency ratio describes relative probability concentration; it does not label one rater more reliable or fair. The saved-fit contrast example keeps joint draws, diagnostics and MCSE together without fitting again.

Ask what a predictive check actually checks

A posterior predictive check generates replicated ratings from the fitted model and compares relevant features with the observations. Examine category use and patterns by rater, item and person, not only an overall average. An aggregate match can hide a systematic mismatch in a subgroup. Prior predictive checks perform the corresponding simulation before conditioning on the observed scores, to inspect the implications of the chosen priors. These operations and their different roles are explained in the Stan predictive-check guide.

Reproducing existing rating rows assesses conditional fit. It does not establish performance on withheld ratings, new people, new items or new raters. Good score predictions also do not demonstrate that separate loadings and abilities are recovered correctly: different parameter combinations may predict similarly.

Compare models against the same prediction task

First state the prediction target. Predicting an additional rating of a known person is different from predicting a new person's work. Then use the same observations, category meanings, split and scoring weights for competing models. If all ratings of a response should be withheld together, keep that response in one fold and check that the remaining training design is still connected and contains the required facet levels.

In K-fold cross-validation, fit each model to the training portion of each fold and evaluate its held-out ratings. A common score is the sum of log predictive probabilities assigned to the observed held-out categories:

\[\widehat{\mathrm{elpd}} =\sum_{n\in\mathrm{heldout}} \log\left[\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S} P(Y_n=y_n\mid\phi^{(s)}_{\mathrm{training}})\right].\]

Higher scores mean better predictions of those outcomes under that scoring rule. Average probabilities before taking their logarithm; averaging log probabilities is a different quantity. This expression scores ratings individually. Grouping ratings into the same fold does not turn the score into the joint predictive density of a whole response. Equal weight per rating also differs from equal weight per person when rating counts differ.

As a deliberately invented example, totals -120 and -116 give a difference of 4 in favor of the second model. The difference alone does not show whether the advantage is reproducible or useful. Examine uncertainty appropriate to the sampling/design structure, failures, diagnostic warnings and the magnitude relevant to your decision. Do not treat overlapping training folds as independent study replications. Predictive superiority also does not prove that the larger model's dimensions represent the intended constructs.

WAIC and importance-sampling LOO estimate predictive quantities from fitted draws under their own assumptions. Importance-sampling instability matters; see the official loo example for reference PSIS-LOO and its diagnostics. The package candidate's raw IS-LOO and experimental Hill-smoothed option must not be presented as independently validated equivalents of that reference implementation.

The current common comparison guide describes saved-fit scoring, explicit refit requests and complete-fold comparison across supported result types. It remains an unreleased candidate. It retains missing folds and warnings, but does not provide automatic multi-model refit execution, inferential K-fold SE/MCSE, automatic winners, or prediction for new facet levels. Model-family, dimension and Q comparisons have distinct compatibility checks. A returned comparison table is not a completed validation of model selection.

Use simulations to plan informative studies

Simulation studies answer different questions depending on how parameters and data are generated:

QuestionSuitable simulationEvidence to inspect
Does the inference procedure reproduce the intended posterior under its own joint prior model?Simulation-based calibration (SBC).Rank behavior across independent prior-generated datasets, accounting for ties and MCMC dependence.
Can particular abilities, loadings or severity contrasts be estimated under a proposed design?Parameter recovery at specified truths and design conditions.Bias, RMSE, interval width/coverage, diagnostics and failure rates, with replication uncertainty.
How often would the proposed design meet a precision or decision requirement?Repeated studies under explicitly justified generating scenarios.The fraction meeting a prespecified criterion, its uncertainty and sensitivity to the scenarios.

In SBC, draw parameters from the actual prior, generate data conditional on them, fit the same model, and locate each generating value among posterior draws. Across independent datasets, ranks should follow the appropriate discrete uniform distribution when computation is calibrated. MCMC dependence and ties need explicit treatment. A flat-looking histogram is a diagnostic, not proof that all errors are absent or the model describes real ratings. See the Stan SBC guide.

For design planning, vary features that can change the intended answer: for example, the number of people, items per dimension, raters per response, linking coverage, or missingness. Specify plausible generating values separately from fitting priors, and evaluate the actual target quantity. Assessing one truth cannot establish performance throughout a population of truths. Prior-averaged SBC calibration does not guarantee nominal coverage at every fixed truth.

Keep failed and incomplete planned datasets in the accounting. If difficult datasets disappear, reported precision and coverage can look better than the procedure actually delivers. The current common recovery functions evaluate saved fits against named truths and summarize fixed-truth replications. They are components for this workflow, not a complete design-planning simulator or completed SBC validation.

Write an answer that matches the evidence

A useful report identifies the question, model family and dimensions, data and rating design, priors and scale constraints, backend and computational controls, diagnostic concerns, and estimates or contrasts with relevant uncertainty. Explain what predictive checks or comparisons tested and what assumptions remain. Retain the saved fit and environment information so that results can be revisited.

For example: “Under the specified two-dimensional model and priors, the communication contrast was positive, with the reported posterior interval. The interval's numerical precision was assessed separately. Prediction was evaluated for withheld ratings of existing persons, items and raters; this does not establish prediction for new persons.” Add the actual values and warnings from the analysis; this is a reporting template, not an empirical finding.

Return to Getting Started, follow the runnable examples, or consult the Bayesian Workflow for the detailed sequence of calls.

For saved alternative loading priors in the normalized MGMFRM candidate, see Would a different prior change my answer? for named contrasts, MCSE, failed variants, interval/ridge views and verified exports.

Bias, reliability and a complete MFRM report

The reporting question should determine the statistic. “Do two raters give the same scores?”, “Can we distinguish persons?”, and “Does a rater treat one group differently?” are different questions. A single coefficient cannot answer all three. The following assessment covers the current development candidate; an available function is not evidence that every model family supports it.

What kind of bias is being investigated?

Separate three meanings:

  • Overall severity/leniency: a rater tends to score everyone lower/higher. This is a modeled facet main effect, not by itself differential bias.
  • Differential facet functioning (DFF): severity depends on an item, group, response type or occasion, after accounting for the intended model. Examples include rater-by-item interactions and rater-by-group contrasts. An observed group mean difference alone is not evidence of bias.
  • Estimator bias: repeated estimates differ systematically from known truth in a recovery study. This is what simulation bias summaries address; it is not evidence that a rater is unfair to an observed group.

dff_report already screens ordinary MFRMFit results. It returns cell counts, expected-score residual contrasts, a local logit approximation, intervals and optional practical-magnitude probabilities. Its scope explicitly excludes fitted DFF effects. The approximation divides score residuals by predictive variance; it is not a new jointly estimated interaction parameter. The existing FACETS procedure also needs to be distinguished: it estimates additional bias terms conditionally after fixing the main estimates. Reproducing that conditional procedure and fitting a fully joint Bayesian interaction model are separate validation tasks.

The default adjustment=:assignment removes a count-weighted additive surface from the observed cell residuals separately in every joint draw. The reference is the observed allocation: each rating has equal weight, so cell weights equal observed counts, including sparse cells. These are not model likelihood weights, inverse-variance weights, or weights for a balanced target population. Missing cells are reported but never imputed. The score-residual surface and the local cell-logit surface are projected separately.

For cell counts [9 1; 1 9], the purely additive cell residual surface [0 0.2; 0 0.2] now yields zero interaction residuals. The historical observed- margin arithmetic gives +0.08 for the first row and -0.08 for the second. This is a deterministic arithmetic check, not an empirical finding or a fitted bias estimate. adjustment=:observed_margins reproduces that historical screen with a warning. Default outputs can change even on a balanced table on the local logit scale, because averaging and division by predictive variance do not commute.

# Existing ordinary MFRM fit; this call does not refit the model.
rows = dff_report(fit_result;
    terms = (:rater, :item), adjustment = :assignment,
    interval = 0.99, expected_score_practical_threshold = 0.2)
# The integrated report forwards the same choice:
report = fit_report(fit_result;
    include_dff = true, dff_terms = (:rater, :item),
    dff_adjustment = :assignment)

Read contrast_status alongside n_observations, assignment_n_components, assignment_additive_rank, assignment_interaction_df and cell_residual_fraction. A tree-shaped observed-cell graph has no independent interaction contrast. A bridge cell attached to a cycle also has no support for its own interaction, even when the whole table has positive interaction degrees of freedom. Such cells retain their raw residuals but have NaN interaction summaries and :no_independent_cell_contrast; a missing cell is :empty_cell. Disconnected components can support contrasts within themselves, not contrasts between components. min_n flags sparse cells rather than silently changing the reference allocation. Rank/support tolerances and numerical zero cleanup concern floating-point arithmetic, not statistical significance or a practical margin.

Intervals propagate the fitted base model's parameter uncertainty conditional on the existing scores and assignment. They are not predictive intervals for another rating study, sampling confidence intervals, or fitted DFF-parameter intervals. Use n_expected_score_dff_draws and n_logit_dff_draws to check availability. An undefined local logit shift in any observed cell makes that entire term's logit projection unavailable for that draw. Partial availability remains flagged; its finite-draw summaries must not conceal missing numerical support. Additive projection does not remove every possible composition effect within cells or identify a causal mechanism, and it supplies no calibrated significance test.

A defensible bias report should specify the contrast and direction, practical margin, logit and/or score-scale effect, interval, cell support and linking, assignment adjustment, prior/shrinkage assumptions, and the complete set of comparisons. If many interactions are screened, marginal posterior probabilities do not automatically provide familywise or false-discovery error control. Unexpected residuals may reflect multidimensionality, dependence or assignment, not a uniquely identified bias mechanism. Neither a flag nor an interval excluding zero by itself establishes unfairness.

For a future fitted interaction API, begin with an identified MFRM interaction model, explicit row/column constraints and partial pooling, then validate sparse and unbalanced designs, reference contrasts, and practical decision accuracy. Group DIF also requires separating group ability distributions from item effects; adding every possible interaction can destroy that identification. Extend to GMFRM/MGMFRM only with a declared response equation, dimension and reference population. Multipliers change the relation between latent contrasts and score changes, so a common raw-logit interpretation cannot be assumed.

Reliability, separation and Strata

The conventional separation summary uses a declared variance of estimated facet measures V and mean squared measurement SE E. Its positive-part convention is A = max(V - E, 0), giving

\[G=\sqrt{A/E},\qquad R=\frac{A}{A+E},\qquad H=\frac{4G+1}{3}.\]

Here G is separation, R separation reliability, and H Strata. For example, G=2 gives R=0.80 and H=3. Specify sample/population variance, error definition, weighting and extreme-score treatment. The FACETS summary definitions make these distinctions. Strata is an approximate resolution index; it does not identify actual latent classes, assign persons to three groups, or establish classification accuracy at a substantive cutoff. Its derivation uses distribution and precision assumptions; see Wright and Masters. A value below one should not be silently relabeled as an established class count.

Rater separation reliability is not inter-rater agreement. High rater separation means severity differences are distinguishable relative to their estimated error. Low separation can reflect similar severities or insufficient information. It is not proof of equivalence. Person separation can be useful for distinguishing people but depends on population spread, design and measurement precision. See the FACETS reliability explanation. Do not equate a GMFRM/MGMFRM consistency multiplier with any of these coefficients.

The current separation_reliability_summary exists for ordinary MFRM, but its historical algorithm is a screen requiring methodological revision, not a validated FACETS or EAP reliability estimator. It computes cross-person variance within each joint posterior draw, subtracts average marginal posterior variance, truncates at zero, and summarizes the ratios. Finite counterexamples expose why that matters:

  • Four artificial draws [-1 -1; -1 1; 1 -1; 1 1] give identical posterior means for both persons, yet reported mean reliability is 1/6, about 0.167.
  • With a person contrast fixed at 2 in every draw, changing only a common random location shift changes reported reliability from about 0.993 to 0.

These are tests of the formula, not fitted posterior samples or a universal replacement definition. They prevent treating the result as an established measure of rank resolution. No reporting Strata API was found, and applying (4G+1)/3 to this historical screen would inherit its unresolved meaning.

The candidate now provides BayesianMGMFRM.posterior_reliability for an explicit finite-cohort target, described below. This does not make it a general population, test–retest or generalizability coefficient. Population reliability requires the corresponding population and response distribution. Nuisance-parameter uncertainty must also be handled; Yang et al. demonstrate why ignoring item-calibration uncertainty can overstate score precision.

For multiple dimensions, report precision/reliability separately by declared dimension. A composite w'θ requires justified weights and full covariance: Var(w'θ | y) = w' Cov(θ | y) w. An average of marginal reliabilities is not a composite reliability. Strata should be offered only for a justified scalar measure with a validated separation definition, not as a single number for an arbitrary multidimensional model.

An explicit posterior reliability target

Choose the people, items or raters whose locations you want to distinguish. For one selected dimension and normalized level weights w_i, let X_i be either the fitted location (target=:absolute) or its difference from the selected cohort's weighted mean in the same joint draw (target=:centered). With m_i = E(X_i | y) and v_i = Var(X_i | y), define

\[\bar m=\sum_i w_i m_i,\qquad B=\sum_i w_i(m_i-\bar m)^2,\qquad W=\sum_i w_i v_i,\qquad R_{\mathrm{cohort}}=\frac{B}{B+W}.\]

This has a direct interpretation by the law of total variance. Independently select a level I with probability w_i and a joint posterior draw. The variance of X_I is exactly B + W: variation in posterior means plus average posterior uncertainty. R_cohort is the fraction due to the first component. The EAP formula in TAM's official documentation has the same ratio structure; this implementation explicitly defines its cohort, centering and joint uncertainty rather than claiming estimator identity.

For target=:centered, let C be the posterior covariance matrix of the selected locations and A = I - 1w'. The transformed covariance is A*C*A'. Centering joint draws computes this transformation implicitly, including all off-diagonal terms. Adding any draw-specific shift to every selected location leaves the centered result unchanged. The absolute result deliberately retains that uncertainty. Neither target is chosen automatically.

Two equal posterior means give B=0 and R_cohort=0 when W>0. If all selected contrasts are exactly fixed in the supplied draws, centering can give W=0 and R_cohort=1; the result flags empirical zero variance and withholds delta MCSE. A constant or stuck sampler is not proof of perfect measurement. If both components are zero, reliability is undefined and returned as missing.

The eight common saved-fit types share one entry. Use the inventory to select estimated locations in a single facet/dimension:

using BayesianMGMFRM
fit_result = load_fit_cache("my-fit.jls") # trusted local cache
catalog = BayesianMGMFRM.recovery_quantities(fit_result)
people = filter(r -> r.block == :person && !r.fixed, catalog.rows)
chosen_dimension = first(people).dimension # inspect its dimension_label first
parameters = [r.parameter for r in people if r.dimension == chosen_dimension]

absolute = BayesianMGMFRM.posterior_reliability(
    fit_result; parameters, target=:absolute,
)
relative = BayesianMGMFRM.posterior_reliability(
    fit_result; parameters, target=:centered,
)
relative.reliability
relative.between_variance
relative.within_variance
relative.mcse
relative.sampling_warning

By default every selected level receives equal weight, regardless of rating count. Optional positive weights follow the order of parameters; changing the selection or weights changes the target. Fixed anchors are rejected rather than treated as perfectly measured members. Derived estimated constraints remain eligible. Loadings, category steps, mixed facets and mixed dimensions are rejected. See examples/saved_posterior_reliability.jl in the candidate source for the cache-only wrapper; this file is not yet released.

The calculation uses normalized probability moments across levels and retained draws, with no sample-variance correction. The returned ratio is one functional of the posterior, not one value per draw from a posterior distribution of reliability. Consequently it has no reported CrI. Its optional numerical MCSE uses a first-order expansion, retaining the original complete chains. If d_i(s)=X_i(s)-m_i, the influence is

\[\psi(s)=\frac{W\,[2\sum_i w_i(m_i-\bar m)d_i(s)] -B\,[\sum_i w_i(d_i(s)^2-v_i)]}{(B+W)^2}.\]

The existing autocorrelation-aware estimator applied to psi supplies MCSE. It is unavailable at B=0 or W=0, with insufficient chains/draws or degenerate influence. It does not remove finite-Monte-Carlo bias, establish convergence, or measure repeated-study uncertainty; no automatic precision cutoff is applied. The saved source/model/prior identities and sampling warnings remain in the output.

A large ratio can reflect a wide selected cohort or informative priors, not just better data. Do not use it alone to rank models, certify validity, plan a new rating design or claim rater agreement. Calibration uncertainty is retained when it is present in the saved joint draws; uncertainty omitted by fixed calibration cannot be recovered here. Strata is intentionally not derived from this ratio. The historical separation_reliability_summary now emits a migration warning; its numerical outputs remain unchanged for reproducibility.

Agreement and practical decisions

rater_homogeneity_summary already provides paired severity contrasts, optional ROPE probabilities and support information for ordinary MFRM. It explicitly states that severity homogeneity is not observed score agreement. It also separates fixed anchors from estimated contrasts. rater_overlap counts shared units; it does not calculate an agreement percentage.

The candidate now provides BayesianMGMFRM.rater_agreement across the eight common saved-fit types. It answers: How often did these raters give the same score to the same response and criterion, and how much agreement does the fitted model expect? It does not ask whether rater severity parameters differ.

agreement = BayesianMGMFRM.rater_agreement(fit_result;
    dataset_id="assessment-2026",
    observation_ids=rating_ids,      # unique rating IDs, in fitted-row order
    response_ids=performance_ids,   # same performance can have several ratings
    adjacent=1, interval=0.99,
    se_unit=:person)
agreement.overall.exact
agreement.rater_pairs
agreement.unmatched_observation_ids

Supply globally unique performance identities from the data collection records; they may repeat across raters and criteria. If the fitted data already carry response_id metadata, omit response_ids. Supplying conflicting identities is an error. The API also checks person and every recorded optional context field, including occasion/task/form, and rejects duplicate response–item–rater rows. Matching by person and item alone can join different performances, so it is never inferred. Unrecorded context cannot be checked; callers must verify that the IDs represent the same performance and scoring occasion. Repeated independent scoring rounds require distinct response/occasion identities, not silent averaging.

Each shared response–item and unordered rater pair contributes one opportunity. Three raters on one criterion create three opportunities. Overall results weight these opportunities equally; they do not average pair percentages equally. Each rater's denominator counts its incident opportunities, so per-rater denominators sum to twice the overall denominator. All fitted rater pairs appear, including zero-overlap pairs with missing rates. The opportunity ledger preserves both rating IDs, scores and response/item/person identity. Thus a selective assignment cannot be mistaken for universal agreement: report coverage and denominators too.

Exact agreement compares original scores. adjacent=1 includes exact agreement and a gap of at most one ordinal category position. A tolerance covering the entire scale always gives one and receives a visible structural flag; it is uninformative about rater agreement. Categories are not recentered at each rater's lowest observed PCM score. This can differ from the category alignment in the FACETS agreement report.

For an opportunity involving raters A and B, each joint posterior draw supplies category probabilities. Conditional independence gives expected exact agreement sum_k p_A(k) * p_B(k); adjacent agreement sums products for all permitted category pairs. Average these probabilities across opportunities within each draw, then summarize those aggregate draws. Shared posterior parameter dependence is retained: if both raters have probabilities (0.9, 0.1) in one draw and (0.1, 0.9) in the other, expected exact agreement is 0.82, whereas multiplying their marginal mean probabilities gives 0.50. The latter answers a different question.

There are three distinct uncertainty statements:

  • expected_lower and expected_upper are equal-tailed posterior intervals for the conditional expected agreement rate for these fitted responses and raters. Choose interval=0.99 for a 99% interval. They are not predictive intervals for realized future agreement or frequentist CIs for the observed rate.
  • mcse reports chain-aware numerical uncertainty in the expected-rate posterior mean and quantiles. Short or degenerate chains keep unavailable flags, alongside the fit's sampling warnings. MCSE does not establish convergence or calibration.
  • Optional se_unit=:person estimates an observed-rate sampling SE by keeping all opportunities from the same person together. With M opportunities, G persons, overall rate p, and person totals z_g = sum_j (I_gj - p), it computes sqrt(G/(G-1) * sum_g z_g^2) / M. This is a ratio-linearization approximation under independent, exchangeable person clusters, conditional on these raters and items. It accommodates unequal numbers of opportunities per person, but does not adjust arbitrary crossed dependence or sampling-design weights. Fewer than two persons is unavailable and zero empirical cluster variance is flagged. The default se_unit=:none makes no observed-rate SE claim.

No observed-rate CI, realized predictive interval, kappa, automatic cutoff or reliability correction is produced. The observed-minus-expected mean difference is descriptive, not a p-value or a chance-corrected agreement coefficient.

Choosing an ICC

An ICC answers a different question from the proportion of exactly matching scores. For example, two raters can rank performances identically while one always awards a point more. Consistency can then be perfect although absolute agreement is lower. Choose the definition from the intended use of the scores, not from which coefficient is larger.

Use BayesianMGMFRM.intraclass_correlation for one explicit item and complete crossed ratings: every response has one rating from the same raters. The initial candidate computes an observed-score ANOVA point estimate, with no fitting and no confidence/credible interval or SE. These choices are required:

ChoiceQuestion answered
definition=:agreementDo scores agree, counting systematic rater offsets?
definition=:consistencyDo scores vary together after removing additive rater offsets?
unit=:singleWhat is the reliability of one rating?
unit=:averageWhat is the reliability of the mean of all K recorded raters?
model=:two_way_randomAre the raters treated as a sample from a larger rater population?
model=:two_way_mixedIs the target this fixed rater panel?
score_scale=:intervalCan equal numerical score gaps be justified substantively?

The fixed/random distinction changes the scope of generalization, while the point formulas for a given definition and unit coincide. The design must justify that interpretation; naming a random model does not establish representative rater sampling. See the psych author's ICC documentation for this distinction and Liljequist et al. (2019) for the ANOVA formulation and systematic rater differences.

The complete no-fit example compares all four definition/unit combinations. The API page shows how to supply response identities and inspect the ANOVA table.

Negative estimates are retained when the denominator is positive. A nonpositive denominator produces an unavailable coefficient with an explicit status; constant scores do not prove reliability. Inspect the returned ANOVA table. The ICC API gives the four equations and the component/denominator interpretation.

The equal-gap assumption is not automatic for ordinal rubric codes. Use the ordinal alpha operation below when agreement under an ordinal distance is the intended question; it is not an interchangeable estimate of the same ICC target. No severity adjustment, trait-dimensional averaging, item pooling, arbitrary future rater-count projection or population reliability guarantee is supplied. The mean-score target refers to the actual K raters, not a planned replacement panel. Interpreting it for future random panels additionally requires the declared sampling model.

Incomplete or uneven rosters and duplicate response-item-rater cells are rejected, without imputation or listwise deletion. Responses shared by raters must refer to the same performance and context, as for score agreement. Multiple performances from one person remain separate response units; n_people and repeated_person_units disclose that dependence. A point estimate is not evidence for independent sampling. Entirely unobserved responses cannot be recovered from these data. One-way ICC, sparse-design mixed models and uncertainty estimation remain outside this candidate's scope.

For a saved fit, put the same choices under reporting.icc with shared dataset and rating IDs. The intraclass_correlation report section includes summary, ANOVA and response-ledger tables in the public JSON/Markdown bundle. The statistic uses ratings rather than posterior draws; computing it does not remove or repair sampler warnings in other sections. Report the design, item, score-scale rationale, all three ICC choices, N/K and absence of an interval alongside the value.

Observed-score alpha

To describe chance-corrected agreement on one criterion, use BayesianMGMFRM.krippendorff_alpha. This reads FacetData directly; it needs no MCMC fit. Select one item label and a measurement level explicitly:

alpha = BayesianMGMFRM.krippendorff_alpha(data;
    item="fluency", level=:ordinal, dataset_id="speaking-study",
    observation_ids=rating_ids, response_ids=response_ids)
alpha.alpha
alpha.status
alpha.marginal_counts
alpha.singleton_observation_ids

Use :nominal when categories distinguish kinds and :ordinal when their declared order is meaningful. Category labels in FacetData are consecutive integers; nominal analysis uses them only as codes. Ordinal analysis does not assume equal numeric gaps. A response ID denotes one performance/occasion and must agree with the recorded person/context. Different items are not pooled; the meaning of the selected criterion still requires substantive justification.

For pairable units, the implementation evaluates alpha = 1 - observed_disagreement / expected_disagreement. With category counts n_cu in unit u and m_u >= 2 ratings, coincidence entries are O_ck = sum_u n_cu * (n_ku - I(c=k)) / (m_u-1). Their margins n_c count pairable ratings, with N = sum_c n_c. Expected coincidences are E_ck = n_c * (n_k - I(c=k)) / (N-1). Each disagreement is the sum of coincidences times squared distances, divided by N. Nominal squared distance is one for different categories and zero otherwise. Ordinal squared distance is the squared difference between cumsum(n_c) - n_c/2 midpoints. This differs from squared category-code distance. See the author's computational reference, sections C–D.

Absent ratings are omitted rows; literal missing scores are not accepted by FacetData. Singletons remain in the returned unit ledger but do not affect either disagreement or the ordinal distances. Entirely unobserved responses cannot be counted without an allocation roster. :no_pairable_units and :zero_expected_disagreement both return missing alpha. Thus identical scores everywhere do not yield an informative reliability coefficient. Negative alpha is retained. The result includes both coincidence matrices and distances for inspection; unlike the exact-agreement rate, it does not weight every rating pair equally when units have different numbers of raters.

For an existing fit, add alpha=(item="fluency", level=:ordinal, response_ids=response_ids) to fit_report's reporting request, with the shared dataset/rating IDs. Its krippendorff_alpha section survives public report save/reload. Sampler warnings remain attached to the fit, while this observed-data coefficient does not depend on its posterior draws. This is not severity-adjusted agreement, an ICC, a posterior reliability ratio or proof of construct validity. No SE, confidence interval, missingness correction or automatic acceptable-value cutoff is provided; population uncertainty needs a separately justified sampling design and resampling method. An observed rate outside the expected-rate posterior interval is not, by itself, a calibrated lack-of-fit test: that interval excludes realized-score variation, and these same ratings were used to fit the model. All original retained draws are used. Explicit prediction-cell and rater-pair budgets reject oversized jobs before extraction; the API never silently samples draws or drops pairs to meet those budgets.

High observed agreement beyond a model's expectation warrants investigation; it does not uniquely diagnose copying or halo. Report uncertainty at the response/person level when pairs share responses. ICC or weighted kappa may be useful complementary summaries when their design, scale and agreement target are appropriate, but cannot be substituted for facet separation. For pass/fail or ranking decisions, report the relevant posterior decision probabilities and conditional precision near the cutoff, not Strata alone.

Category functioning across model families

Use BayesianMGMFRM.category_report to ask whether the declared categories are used, what their transitions mean in this model, and how category probabilities change for a specified item and rater. It reads the eight common saved-fit types without refitting. The ownership of a step is determined by the fitted model; the report cannot change that ownership after estimation.

Fitted category modelOwner of centered category stepsConditional adjacent-category crossing
Ordinary MFRM rating scaleShared across items and ratersItem position + rater severity + shared step

These are the implemented families, not every possible PCM/GPCM specification. In scalar GMFRM, the item-discrimination and rater-consistency product multiplies the whole adjacent-category contrast. For given position/step values it changes steepness, not the crossing. MGMFRM instead puts item loadings on abilities and uses the literal 1.7 rater scale; fixed-Q MFRM retains its unit scale. These conventions and centering constraints are recorded in model_contract and step_contract. Compare models only after checking their category equations and identification.

usage_rows retain every declared category overall, per item and per rater, including unused categories. Counts use actual rating rows as the denominator; they do not give equal weight to persons or correct an unbalanced allocation. The threshold_levels field lists the step-owner groups that actually contributed to each row. For example, a GMFRM item row can combine several raters' different step systems. Rater differences in raw category use can reflect the abilities and items allocated to them, so they do not alone diagnose centrality or bias.

Each row's expected summary describes the posterior distribution of its conditional expected category proportion. Its SD and interval propagate parameter uncertainty. They omit the additional variation from realizing a new set of ratings. By contrast, the existing ordinary-MFRM category_functioning_summary simulates replicated category proportions and retains that predictive interpretation. The two intervals answer different questions; observed-minus-expected is neither a p-value nor a calibrated lack-of-fit test.

threshold_rows contain the centered transition parameters, excluding the dummy reference-category zero, with posterior SD, pointwise equal-tailed intervals, joint-draw step gaps and ordering probabilities. Posterior SD is not a repeated-sampling SE. A reversed pair is a review prompt, not an automatic category-collapse rule. Constant sampled ordering indicators do not establish zero Monte Carlo error or certain ordering. Check mcse, mcse_warning and sampling_warning alongside any summary.

curve_rows are plot-ready pointwise category probabilities. A profile is a slice at the stated other abilities, not an average over them or a population curve. A grid does not establish empirical support. To compare items or raters, add separately named profiles; preserve their conditions in the figure caption, outside the image. An item that does not load on the plotted dimension has a flat curve and no unique crossing along that dimension.

crossing_rows calculate adjacent equal-probability ability values in each joint posterior draw before summarizing. This preserves associations among positions, steps and loadings; a ratio of posterior means would generally differ. With three or more categories, equality of two adjacent probabilities need not mean that either probability is 0.5. Centered steps, these conditional crossings and expected-score half-points are distinct quantities.

interval=0.99 applies a pointwise 99% equal-tailed interval to expected use, steps, gaps, probabilities and crossings. This API does not calculate HDIs or simultaneous bands. Results condition on the fitted model and its calibration policy; fixed anchors omit external calibration uncertainty. All original draws, chain layout, observation IDs, source identity and warnings are retained. Resource limits fail explicitly rather than selecting a subset of draws. The optional fit_report(...; reporting=(...)) integration retains these definitions in a separate category_report section. Neither entry point establishes category calibration, a new-rater guarantee or model validity.

Reading one integrated report

Use the Saved-fit Reporting API to include category, reliability and agreement results in one fit_report bundle. The integration retains each statistic's target and uncertainty definition: expected category-use CrIs omit realization noise; a reliability estimate has numerical MCSE but no reliability CrI or Strata; agreement separates observed rates/optional observed-rate SE from conditional expected-rate posterior intervals. A common report format does not make these uncertainty measures interchangeable.

Named reliability cohorts are evaluated separately. A failed cohort does not erase successful ones; its failure makes report execution incomplete. Inspect the named error rows in a partially failed report before correcting a request. A report can also be execution-complete while containing MCMC warnings, unavailable MCSE, zero-overlap pairs or unrequested analyses. Inspect both section status and quantity-level warnings before interpreting results. Posterior classification and conditional ability precision

A practical question is: Does this person's ability meet the standard, and how uncertain is that judgment given these ratings? This differs from asking whether a method achieves an acceptable false-pass rate across repeated studies. BayesianMGMFRM.posterior_classification addresses the first question using an existing fit. It requires two distinct choices: the substantive ability cutoff c and the probability rule t used to report a pass.

For person i, let q_i = Pr(theta_i >= c | ratings, model, prior). The function estimates q_i by the fraction of their saved ability draws at or above c. It reports pass when q_i >= t, otherwise fail. Equality belongs to the qualifying/passing side. This is an explicit reporting rule, not an automatically recommended standard. Both c and t are required; the cutoff uses the exact fitted coordinate system, including its identification constraints. The Stan guide's event-probability example explains estimation by posterior indicator averages.

Reported quantityInterpretation
Ability mean, SD and equal-tailed intervalConditional ability estimate and uncertainty, including calibration uncertainty represented in the saved joint draws. SD is not numerical MCSE or a repeated-sampling standard error.
Qualification probabilityPosterior probability of ability at or above the specified cutoff.
Error probability for a displayed pass1 - q_i, the posterior probability that this person is below the cutoff.
Error probability for a displayed failq_i, the posterior probability that this person is at or above the cutoff.
Expected false-pass / false-fail countsSums of the relevant individual error probabilities, each selected person counted once.
Probability of any errorPosterior probability that at least one displayed decision is wrong, retaining dependence among people.

For example, if q_i=0.92 and the required probability is t=0.90, the decision is pass and its posterior error probability is 0.08. With t=0.95, it is fail and its error probability is 0.92. The latter is possible because a strict pass rule can prioritize avoiding false passes. The API does not infer those costs, select the rule, or certify it as optimal. An indeterminate/review category is not part of this binary interface.

Cohort risk must respect shared uncertainty. Consider two people with qualification probabilities of 0.75, both reported as pass. The expected error count is 0.50 regardless of dependence. But if their below-cutoff events always occur together, the probability of any error is 0.25; if the events never occur together, it is 0.50. Multiplying marginal probabilities would produce a misleading independence-based value. The implementation evaluates the decisions against each complete joint draw and only then summarizes the cohort.

Inspect probability MCSE as well as the saved convergence diagnostics. A choice near t can change with numerical noise. Constant sampled indicators retain probabilities of zero or one but unavailable MCSE; they do not establish certainty. A false-pass count is structurally zero if nobody was passed, and a false-fail count is structurally zero if everybody was passed. Count/probability MCSE conditions on the displayed decisions; it does not account for changing those decisions in a different finite MCMC run. No automatic precision gate or refit is applied. The Stan reference manual distinguishes posterior uncertainty from simulation error with dependent draws.

Select exact estimated person coordinates in one dimension. Another dimension needs its own substantive scale and standard; separate marginal calls do not define a combined, compensatory or conjunctive decision. Fixed anchors are not uncertain people and are rejected. The API does not center the selected cohort, link models, rank people, infer an ability-precision curve over unobserved levels, or predict new ratings. Shared calibration uncertainty absent from the fit cannot be restored by this report.

Finally, these posterior risks condition on the fitted model, prior and observed data. They are not observed mistakes against known truth, population sensitivity or specificity, repeated-study coverage, or future-design error rates. Those claims need suitable validation data or a simulation with known truth and an explicit target population. The current unfavorable loading-recovery finding is not repaired by adding this reporting API; the model's scientific acceptance remains separate.

Posterior ranks within an explicit cohort

A rank answers a relative question: Among these people, who is likely to have higher ability on this dimension? It does not answer whether someone meets an absolute standard or whether two abilities differ enough to matter in practice. Use the classification report for a justified absolute cutoff and inspect ability differences when substantive magnitude matters.

BayesianMGMFRM.posterior_ranking requires exact person coordinates and an explicit direction. With :higher, the largest ability is rank 1; with :lower, the smallest is rank 1. Only the selected people compete for these positions. Adding or removing a person changes the estimand; the result is not a population percentile. A multidimensional fit can be inspected one dimension at a time, but the API does not supply an overall multidimensional ranking.

The function ranks each complete posterior draw and summarizes the resulting ranks. This preserves shared calibration uncertainty represented in the fit and posterior dependence between people. Sorting each person's marginal samples separately would destroy that dependence. It also differs from ranking posterior means. For example, across four equally weighted joint draws, let A have abilities [1000, 0, 0, 0], B [1, 1, 1, 1], and C [2, 2, 2, 2]. A has the largest mean ability but is first in only one draw and last in three, giving expected rank 2.5. The expected ranks of B and C are 2.25 and 1.25. This deliberately extreme finite example shows the distinction, not a fitted model recommendation. Posterior functions and event indicators can be averaged over draws as described in the Stan guide.

OutputWhat it means
Mean rank and rank SDExpected relative position and its conditional posterior spread, using the declared tie policy. An expected rank need not be an integer.
Rank median and pointwise intervalQuantiles of that person's sampled midrank distribution. Empirical inverse-CDF endpoints remain on observed rank support; no interpolation invents unsupported fractional positions.
Mean-rank MCSENumerical uncertainty in the estimated expected rank, accounting for chain autocorrelation. It is not the rank SD.
Top-k lower / upper probabilitiesBounds on inclusion under unspecified tie-breaking at the kth position. These coincide if no sampled tie crosses that boundary.
Tie probabilityFraction of draws in which this person shares an ability value with someone in the cohort; the numerical equality test is exact.

For a tied group occupying positions a through b, every member receives midrank (a+b)/2. Suppose A and B tie for first while C is third, and only one place is available. A and B each have midrank 1.5, but their top-one inclusion probabilities depend on the tie-breaking rule. The lower bound for each is zero and the upper is one. The report does not silently grant each probability 0.5 or choose by input order. If two places are available, both are included. The API counts inclusion as certain in a draw when the tied group's last position is at most k, and possible when its first position is at most k. The difference between the two posterior probabilities is the probability of a boundary-crossing tie. This range is not a credible interval. Near-equal floating-point values are ordered numerically; exact ties do not implement a practical-equivalence margin. No selection action is performed.

Marginal rank intervals need not form one consistent ordering, can overlap, and are not simultaneous uncertainty bounds for the whole cohort. With discrete ranks, their empirical probability mass may exceed the requested level. The mean rank need not lie inside its equal-tailed interval. Rank-interval/quantile MCSE is not computed; its absence is explicitly recorded. Short or constant sampled ranks/events also have unavailable mean MCSE rather than established certainty. Requesting top_k equal to cohort size is instead the structural event that everybody is included. Read diagnostics and event MCSE before interpreting small differences between reported probabilities.

These results are conditional on the fitted model, prior and data, for the specified observed cohort. They do not establish known-truth ranking accuracy, future-performance rankings or selection performance under a new design. Ranks can be unstable even when ability differences are negligible; conversely, a stable ordering does not establish a practically important gap. Model and prior sensitivity, scientific acceptance, and substantive decision criteria remain separate from successful rank reporting. No new MCMC is required to change the reported cohort, direction, interval level or top-k query.
