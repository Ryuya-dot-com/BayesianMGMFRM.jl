Paired ordinal ratings with event association

Use BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation to describe two ordinal traits measured on the same productions, while separating variation between persons, between productions, and within an assessment event. For example, multiple listeners rate a recording for accentedness and comprehensibility. A recording is a response; one listener assessing it is an event. Repeated listeners do not become additional independent speakers.

This is an opt-in Experimental model with unit coefficients, explicit criterion means, estimated person SDs and a common ordinal association parameter. It is separate from the fixed-person-SD JointRatings model. No scientific default prior, general construct-validation claim or causal interpretation is supplied. Its first pronunciation application retains joint/grouped misfit and unassessed prior sensitivity despite adequate numerical precision. A numerically successful fit can be substantively inadequate.

Specify the observations and priors

Use the JointRatings input schema: observation_id, person, task, response_id, rating_event_id, rater, criterion, and integer raw score. IDs are exact nonempty strings. Each criterion declares its full increasing integer levels, direction and threshold_group. Use two distinct threshold groups and a common number of at least three categories. Omit a missing rating; its partner remains in the likelihood. Both criteria must occur for each person, and at least two persons, tasks and raters are required. These structural checks do not establish statistical identification.

using BayesianMGMFRM
const A = BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation

# Illustrative working prior, not a recommended default. Choose and assess it
# for your population, design, category support and substantive question.
prior = (
    task_sd=1.5, rater_sd=(1.0, 1.0), step_sd=(1.0, 1.0),
    response_log_sd_mean=(log(.5), log(.5)), response_log_sd_sd=(.5, .5),
    person_lkj_eta=2.0, response_lkj_eta=2.0,
    intercept_sd=1.5, person_log_sd_mean=log(.5), person_log_sd_sd=.5,
    lambda_sd=.5,
)
target = A.model(rows; criteria, prior)
A.contract()

Criterion-specific vectors follow sorted criterion IDs; step SDs follow sorted threshold-group IDs. Task, rater and step effects use orthonormal sum-zero contrasts. Their declared SDs describe contrast coordinates, not the marginal SD of every centered effect. Person effects have a zero population mean; their realized sample mean is uncertain. Independent criterion means have Normal(0, intercept_sd²) priors. Person and response log SDs have the specified normal priors; both correlation matrices have the specified LKJ priors. Lambda has a Normal(0, lambda_sd²) prior. There is no silently inserted intercept, predictor, freely estimated slope or rater-specific lambda.

Before relying on results, examine the implications of these priors, fit and sensitivity for your application. The package's structural and numerical checks do not supply that assessment. Large repeated-rating counts cannot compensate for a small number of persons when interpreting population covariance.

Fit and preserve the uncertainty

result = A.fit(target; ndraws=4000, warmup=1500, chains=4,
    seed=7281, reconstruction_seed=7282, target_accept=.95,
    max_depth=12, metric=:diagonal)
save_fit("paired-ordinal.jls", result)

Calling fit starts sampling; constructing model does not. Counts above are illustrative, not a guarantee of adequate precision. The analytic gradient feeds AdvancedHMC. Threaded chains can be selected with chain_execution=:threads, max_parallel_chains=4 in a process with enough Julia threads. No automatic continuation or fit-cache lookup occurs. There is no public CmdStan sampling backend for this route; independent fixed-point Stan checks do not constitute a posterior or speed comparison.

Sampling integrates the common person-mean/intercept decomposition exactly. For each retained joint state, fit draws two independent conditional white coordinates using reconstruction_seed and stores them. They restore the uncertainty in person profiles and criterion means. Saving or reloading never replaces them with conditional expectations or regenerates them. All parameters, chain/iteration ordering, warmup telemetry and warnings are retained together.

Native .jls files are trusted-input files requiring their producing source, Julia and Project/Manifest environment. Do not load untrusted native files. A newer source is not automatically compatible. An audited migration must check the original source, joint pairing and reconstructed effects; it is separate from ordinary load_result. JSON reports do not contain a replayable posterior. Existing paths are not overwritten.

Reopen, report and interpret

saved = load_fit("paired-ordinal.jls")
summary = posterior_summary(saved; interval=.95)
save_report("paired-summary.json", summary)

# Use your exact stored criterion and person IDs.
difference = A.contrast(saved; facet=:person, criterion="Accentedness",
    left="speaker:201", right="speaker:202", interval=.95)
save_report("speaker-difference.json", difference)

Reports retain model/prior metadata and contextual warnings. Default summaries cover person profiles, criterion means, person/response SDs and the four association quantities below. Exact names in summary.result.parameter_names can be selected with parameters=[...]. All raw, model and conditional-location diagnostics remain even when fewer parameters are summarized. Check R-hat, bulk/tail ESS, retained divergences, energy diagnostics, warmup warnings and the MCSE of both means and interval endpoints. Focal precision is explicitly unassessed until compared against the precision required by your decision. Intervals are equal-tailed credible intervals, not HDIs or raw-score intervals.

Reported quantityInterpretation
rho_personModel population correlation between the two person effects; depends on the population model and prior.
centered_profile_correlationCorrelation across the observed persons' latent profiles in each joint draw; a finite-sample description.
rho_responseCorrelation between produced-response effects after adjusting for person, task and rater effects.
lambdaConditional adjacent log odds ratio between the two ordinal scores within an event; not a Pearson correlation.

A person contrast compares the same trait across two persons. Positive values mean more of the canonical trait; raw-score direction is retained. Positive rater contrasts mean greater severity. Contrasting one person's accentedness against their comprehensibility as if they were the same scale is unsupported. Differences between raw-score correlation and rho_person are differences in estimands, not evidence that one number corrects the other.

For one existing event, inspect the full conditional table:

joint = A.event_probabilities(saved; rating_event_id="your-event-id")
save_report("event-probabilities.json", joint)

The table supplies original raw labels, intervals and MCSE. It conditions on fitted effects and includes both margins even for a singleton observation. These probabilities are not heldout performance estimates or new-person predictions. Positive probability mass is not evidence against a zero effect. There is no automatic adequacy decision. The existing application's joint and rater-group discrepancies remain a reason to limit interpretation, not a reason to declare a psychological mechanism or automatically select a richer model.

Model and numerical reference

For canonical scores k,l=0,…,K−1, c=(K−1)/2 and criterion-specific adjacent logit sums U, the normalized table is

\[p(k,l\mid\cdot)=\frac{\exp\{U_1(k)+U_2(l)+\lambda(k-c)(l-c)\}} {\sum_{a=0}^{K-1}\sum_{b=0}^{K-1}\exp\{U_1(a)+U_2(b)+\lambda(a-c)(b-c)\}}.\]

Each location is criterion mean + person − task − rater + response effect. The association term follows the fixed-score uniform-association construction described by Goodman (1979). Its combination with these hierarchies and priors is this model's specification, not a prior recommendation by that paper. Lambda=0 gives conditional independence. Nonzero lambda changes the margins; this is not a copula preserving the independent PCM margins. Missing partners are marginalized from the same joint table under an ignorable-missingness assumption.

The selected nine-category model has independent normalized-density and gradient agreement with a complete Stan reference at finite points, and preserves an existing four-chain Julia analysis through an audited saved-result transfer. This is implementation evidence, not general recovery, SBC, prior robustness, independent scientific acceptance, Stan posterior agreement or ConQuest native equivalence. Three-category deterministic/sampler checks establish a separate small operation test, not additional empirical validation.

Help

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.contract — Function
contract()

Experimental two-criterion ordinal association model. Unit coefficients; estimated person SDs, explicit criterion means, centered task effects and correlated response effects. Lambda is a uniform adjacent log odds ratio, not a Pearson correlation or a psychological halo. No predictors, new-level prediction, time effects, scientific default prior or acceptance are supplied. Native samples require their producing source and environment; JSON is a report.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.model — Function
model(rows; criteria, prior)

Use the JointRatings row/criterion schema, with separate threshold groups and the same full support size (at least three categories). Omit missing rows. Response IDs identify productions; event IDs identify a rater's assessment of a production. They are separate namespaces, and a singleton event is retained.

Declare exactly task_sd, two rater_sd, two step_sd, two response_log_sd_mean, two response_log_sd_sd, person_lkj_eta, response_lkj_eta, intercept_sd, person_log_sd_mean, person_log_sd_sd and lambda_sd. Criterion vectors and step groups follow sorted IDs. SDs must be positive. Intercepts have independent N(0,interceptsd²) priors. Both person log SDs have independent N(personlogsdmean,personlogsdsd²) priors. Lambda has N(0,lambdasd²). All priors are proper and normalized in the sampled white/log-SD/Fisher-z coordinates; no prior is silently adopted.

The likelihood is proportional to exp(U₁(k)+U₂(l)+lambda(k-c)(l-c)), c=(K-1)/2, normalized over K² cells. Nonzero lambda changes the margins. Person sample means are integrated during sampling and conditionally restored, not constrained to zero. Validation does not establish model adequacy.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.fit — Function
fit(target; ndraws, warmup, chains, seed, reconstruction_seed, kwargs...)

Run AdvancedHMC with an analytic gradient. Explicit independent seeds identify sampling and conditional-location restoration. Store both raw joint draws and the two conditional white values per draw; reloading never regenerates them. Controls include target_accept=.9, max_depth=10, step_size=.03, init_jitter=.1, metric=:diagonal, chain_execution=:serial, max_parallel_chains=chains and progress=false. There is no automatic refit, extension or scientific acceptance. Inspect report and save the result.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.report — Function
report(result; parameters=nothing, interval=.95)

Summarize named person profiles, person/response correlations and SDs, criterion means, lambda and centered-profile correlation. parameters selects exact stored names (e.g. word[...], rater[...]) for summaries/MCSE; diagnostics always retain every raw, model and conditional-location parameter. Intervals are equal-tailed. Mean and endpoint MCSE are reported, never auto-qualified. Whole-source sampler/warmup warnings and saved contextual limitations remain. Population correlation, finite-profile correlation, response correlation and the local log odds ratio answer different questions; none establishes causality.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.contrast — Function
contrast(result; facet, criterion, left, right, interval=.95, rope=nothing)

Same-criterion :person or :rater difference, left minus right, with equal-tailed interval, MCSE and all source diagnostics. Positive means higher canonical trait for persons, greater severity for raters. Exact string IDs are required. This is not a raw-score or across-criterion contrast. No refit or conditional RNG occurs.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.event_probabilities — Function
event_probabilities(result; rating_event_id, interval=.95)

Joint category probabilities at one existing rating event, conditional on fitted effects. Returns canonical cells with both original raw labels, equal-tailed intervals and MCSE. A singleton still has a full joint table. This is an in-sample conditional description, not heldout prediction or a product of marginals.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.save_result — Function

save_result(path, result) validates and saves without overwrite. Trusted native format only.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.load_result — Function

load_result(path) validates source/environment, joint pairing and every density; no RNG or fit.

BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.JointRatings.OrdinalAssociation.save_report — Function

save_report(path, report) writes portable JSON without overwrite; it contains no replayable posterior.
