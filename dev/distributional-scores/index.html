Explain dispersion and endpoint use

Use this scalar ZOIB extension when a condition or listener attribute may change rating variability or endpoint use, in addition to the interior mean. Start with the bounded-score guide. Keep its mean model and replace observation_prior with blocks such as:

observation_prior = (
    precision=(intercept=LogNormal(log(12), .35),
        predictors=(response=(condition=Normal(0, .7),),),
        population=(assessor=LogNormal(log(.2), .35),)),
    endpoint_logit=(intercept=Normal(-1, .6),
        predictors=(response=(condition=Normal(0, .7),),),
        population=(assessor=.15,)),
    upper_logit=(intercept=Normal(0, .6),
        predictors=(response=(condition=Normal(0, .7),),),
        population=(assessor=.15,)))

Pass this value to the same mfrm_spec call. assessor must be a facet name already declared in that call; it is not a built-in role. Add a numeric condition column to the input. The values illustrate syntax, not recommended priors. A scalar prior entry still declares a constant parameter. A structured entry requires intercept; predictors and population are optional.

What each block means

BlockLinkPositive coefficient means
precisionlog beta concentrationLess interior variability at a fixed interior mean
endpoint_logitlogit probability of either endpointMore endpoint observations
upper_logitlogit probability of the upper endpoint, conditional on an endpointMore upper rather than lower endpoints

Every coefficient and random intercept in these blocks adds on its link scale, including listener-owned terms. Mean-model facet severity still subtracts from the interior-mean predictor. Do not transfer that sign convention here. upper_logit does not directly model the unconditional upper-endpoint probability: if endpoint probability is z and conditional upper probability is h, the latter probability is zh. Interior beta variance on the normalized scale is mu(1-mu)/(precision+1). Higher precision need not reduce the whole mixture's SD.

Use person, response, or any declared facet for predictor ownership. Attributes owned by a person or facet must be constant within that owner; session-varying attributes belong at the appropriate response/session level. Response predictors can use the existing (prior=Normal(...), by=:facet_name) declaration. Population intercepts may be declared for a person or named facet, with a fixed positive SD or a LogNormal SD prior. Owner counts are not capped at five facets.

Blocks have separate, independent Gaussian random intercepts. Reusing an owner name does not share a residual across blocks. Cross-block correlations, slopes within these blocks, multiple mean slopes per owner, and joint outcomes are not provided here. The declared mean intercept/slope pair can coexist with these distributional intercepts.

Check information before interpreting coefficients

Run validate_design(spec) before fitting. Full-design aliases are rejected. Reports also show contributing rows and fixed-design ranks: precision uses interior observations; the conditional-upper block uses endpoint observations; the endpoint block uses all observations. Missing conditional directions trigger warnings while proper priors keep the target defined. Full rank is not proof of adequate information: rare endpoints or few owners may leave large uncertainty and strong prior sensitivity. A positive SD interval under a LogNormal prior does not test whether a variance is zero.

Fit and inspect the usual prior/PPC, convergence and trace reports. PPC should look at variability and endpoint use across the predictor of interest, as well as pooled summaries; a mean-only check can miss these differences. Preserve failed predictive replicas rather than squeezing endpoints or replacing draws.

report = predict(result; rows=[1, 2], metrics=(
    :precision, :endpoint_probability,
    :upper_given_endpoint_probability, :score_sd))
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(report; kind=:prediction)
save_report("distribution-parameters.json", report)

These intervals describe posterior uncertainty in conditional distribution parameters. score_sd includes interior and endpoint mixture variation in the original score units; interior_sd excludes endpoint observations. Neither is an interval for a future individual score. Default predict metrics remain total mean, interior mean, and unconditional lower/upper probabilities. Predictions condition on known owners and preserve their stored attributes.

Use parameter_names(result) for coefficient and SD names, for example coefficient[precision,response,condition] and residual_sd[precision,assessor]. The same names select scientific factors in prior_sensitivity; prediction estimands accept the metrics above. The precision intercept prior is on positive concentration, with its transformation Jacobian retained; coefficient priors are on the stated links. Reweighting a scientific prior does not power coordinate Jacobians. Review weight reliability and MCSE before drawing sensitivity conclusions.

criteria(result; unit=:rating) can compare this model with constant blocks on the same observed ratings, bounds and measure. This is known-owner predictive scoring, not prediction for an unseen listener or a general superiority claim. Save/reopen uses the existing fit/report operations.

The observation mixture and distributional regression match the corresponding brms family definition and distributional-regression framework. Posterior equivalence additionally requires matching predictors, signs, all priors and identification. A paper's more extensive correlated model is not reproduced by this reduced declaration alone.
