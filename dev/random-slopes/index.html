Allow a predictor's effect to vary by listener

Use this Experimental extension when listeners may respond differently to speech rate or another numeric predictor. A common rate coefficient describes the population association; random slopes describe remaining listener variation. A listener-experience interaction explains part of that variation if declared separately. None of these terms is a rater discrimination parameter.

Declare the pair in the existing population block

Start from the bounded-score example. Replace the listener's population SD with an intercept/slope declaration; keep the other owners and the common coefficient:

using Distributions: Normal, LogNormal, LKJ

population = (person=LogNormal(log(.5), .35), recording_effect=.3,
    listener_effect=(intercept=LogNormal(log(.4), .35),
        slopes=(rate_change=LogNormal(log(.6), .4),),
        correlation=LKJ(2, 2.0)))
predictors = (person=(mean_rate=Normal(0, .6),),
    response=(rate_change=Normal(0, .6),))

spec = mfrm_spec(ratings;
    person=:speaker, score=:score, performance=:recording,
    facets=(recording_effect=:recording, listener_effect=:listener),
    observation=(family=:zero_one_inflated_beta, bounds=(0., 100.)),
    observation_prior=(precision=LogNormal(log(12), .4),
        endpoint_logit=Normal(-2, .6), upper_logit=Normal(0, .7)),
    population, predictors, intercept=Normal(0, 1),
    decompose=(rate=(between=:mean_rate, within=:rate_change),))
validate_design(spec)

The values illustrate syntax, not universal priors. Input rate is constant within each recording. Decomposition uses each speaker's unique performances, not the number of ratings. Scalar ZOIB and scalar count (binomial and beta_binomial) models accept one or more declared mean slopes per named owner. Names remain user-defined; multiple owners may declare slopes, subject to design and numerical checks. Ordinal random slopes, distributional-block slopes, new-owner integration and joint outcomes remain separate extensions.

correlation=0 fixes independence. With K slopes on an owner, LKJ(K + 1, eta) estimates the residual (K + 1) × (K + 1) correlation matrix across the intercept and declared slopes; larger eta concentrates more prior mass near zero. No correlation prior is chosen silently. Intercept and slope SDs may each be fixed positive numbers or LogNormal priors. Correlated residuals are noncentered internally; this computation does not change the declared joint prior.

Interpret signs, units and uncertainty

For a listener facet, the model subtracts both its intercept and its slope contribution. With common coefficient beta and listener slope residual b1, the listener's interior-mean response slope is beta − b1. For a person-owned slope, its contribution is positive. A positive listener slope residual therefore reduces the rate association. The estimated correlation concerns residual intercepts/slopes after any declared owner predictors, not the sample correlation of posterior-mean listener scores.

After fitting, use exact names from parameter_names(result) to combine a facet's slope with its common coefficient, for example for integer listener ID 2:

contrast(result;
    left="coefficient[response,rate_change]",
    right="slope[listener_effect,rate_change,2]")

This computes beta minus b1 within each joint draw, retaining their dependence and reporting the interval and its Monte Carlo uncertainty. Subtracting marginal interval endpoints would not give this interval. This difference is a total response slope for a facet; a person-owned slope instead adds to beta.

The slope refers to the interior-mean logit, not the original 0–100 mean. Use predict(result) for the expected score including endpoint probabilities. Its intervals describe uncertainty in expectations, not future individual scores. Known-owner predictions preserve stored performance/source attributes; changing an observed recording's rate is not a supported counterfactual operation.

Centering changes the meaning of the intercept and its covariance with slopes. Changing units rescales slopes and their SDs. To preserve a model under either change, transform the joint prior as well; assigning the same independent priors again specifies a different model. An estimated random-slope variance with only a few listeners may remain strongly prior-sensitive. Structural kernel checks can expose aliasing but do not guarantee practical identification. Because a LogNormal SD prior excludes zero, a positive SD credible interval is not evidence against an intercept-only model. Use an explicitly matched model comparison for that predictive question, retaining its diagnostics and limits.

Use the same workflow

Call prior_predictive_check, fit, qualify, fit_report, predictive_check, predict, plot, save_fit and save_report as in the bounded guide. parameter_names(result) lists slope[owner,column,ID], slope_sd[owner,column] and correlation[owner,column] alongside the existing parameters. Inspect full numerical diagnostics and focal mean/interval-endpoint MCSE; visually mixed traces alone do not establish precision or model fit. Extreme beta draws can round to an endpoint numerically. Predictive checks record failed replications without clipping or replacement. If replications_complete is false, displayed intervals condition on the representable replicas; they cannot establish adequacy for the full predictive distribution. Inspect numerical_failures and warnings.

logprior(result) exposes the slope-SD and correlation factors for prior_sensitivity. Power the scientific factors; conditional residuals and Jacobians stay unchanged. LKJ(1) is uniform in correlation for this pair, so its power scaling is exactly unchanged: a flat sensitivity curve at LKJ(1) cannot establish robustness to alternative correlation priors. Near-one screens also cannot establish broad prior robustness. Keep poor-weight warnings.

Known-owner rating-wise criteria(...; unit=:rating) retains the same observation measure and counts each held response once. It does not evaluate a new listener, speaker or recording. For precision/endpoint regressions alongside the mean slope, see distributional regressions. Joint outcomes and population-integrated prediction remain separate capabilities.

The covariance separation follows the Stan hierarchical-model formulation and LKJ specification. The analogous brms grouping expression is (1 + x | listener), after aligning signs, predictor transformations and all priors. Similar syntax or likelihoods do not establish posterior equivalence.
