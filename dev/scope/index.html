Scope and Releases

Local development scope — 19 October 2026

This manual includes unreleased interfaces and examples. It is not the documentation of an installed release. Model support and scientific acceptance remain as stated on each page.

BayesianMGMFRM.jl follows a conservative release policy: a model family is documented as supported only when its likelihood, parameterization, fitting path, diagnostics, and user-facing examples are covered together.

Current Support

For promotion of an experimental route to Supported, the selected release must specify the exact model/prior/backend and advertised operations, establish their mathematical and claim-appropriate statistical evidence, verify the complete public workflow (including figures and saved reproduction) in the declared environments, and resolve independent scientific/user review before finalizing its API and compatibility policy. These are the existing acceptance conditions, not a requirement to finish every possible model combination. Support describes that bounded promise; it is not a guarantee that any supplied data support the construct, that every fit is adequate or that intervals are calibrated under every data-generating process.

The normalized independent D2 route has bounded scientific and actual-fit evidence; integrated environment acceptance and independent review remain. The scalar design-planning route connects preparation, explicit execution, full-roster summaries and saved reproduction. Fixed-truth target-attainment and joint-prior SBC teaching studies now exercise these operations. Their evidence is limited to the declared model, prior, allocation, quantities and precision objectives: neither an unmet width target nor a rank screen supports a general design recommendation or a calibration guarantee. Rank comparisons retain their independence and posterior-stationarity assumptions. The design-planning route remains experimental; independent workflow review and acceptance of the advertised environment and compatibility scope still precede promotion. See the design-planning guide for the reproducible procedure and its limits.

Experimental features may change in a minor release and should be used with sensitivity checks. They must not be described as stable equivalents of external software or as evidence for broader MGMFRM support. The namespace is an explicit experimental stability boundary, not a maturity claim. BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.surface_contract() records its exact current family configurations and constraints. For slope restrictions, inspect surface_contract(model; prior) instead of inheriting a family-level backend list. Unwrapped normalized, slope-restricted and mean-profile models all return NormalizedMGMFRMFit; distinguish their priors and coefficient policies using fit_metadata, not the result type alone. The historical experimental = true keyword is a compatibility route and does not define the forward-looking API.

For their exact parameter meanings and runnable entry points, see exchangeable rater priors, normalized MGMFRM priors and the normalized fitting example. The two uses of "exchangeable" above are different prior types for different models. Neither estimates a population rater SD from the data.

Common operations and model-specific limits

For tested Julia/environment combinations, native-cache restrictions and the steps to reopen or share an analysis, see the saved-result reproduction guide. The declared Julia minimum is not a guarantee for every environment or saved format.

Experimental.JointRatings has distinct model/result contracts. Use the shared load_fit, posterior_summary and save_report verbs for persistence/reporting; its specialized predict and score operations retain their documented targets. Its records are not accepted by the common fit_report/load_fit_cache workflow or the comparison and recovery functions in the table below. A computed response score is not an accepted heldout model comparison.

The candidate connects eight result types to common saved-result operations: MFRMFit, GMFRMFit, MGMFRMFit, Experimental.NormalizedMGMFRMFit, Experimental.CorrelatedMGMFRMFit, MultidimensionalMFRMFit, Experimental.CorrelatedMFRMFit and Experimental.ExchangeableMFRMFit. An operation being available does not establish that every model is appropriate for the same research question. Unsupported combinations remain unsupported.

The 3D response-surface API specifically accepts independent MGMFRMFit results with GeneralizedPrior and at least two dimensions. It does not accept normalized, slope-restricted, mean-profile or correlated fits, even where their numerical prediction and posterior interval summaries are available. Plot support is separate from fit and common-report support.

Start with the saved comparison and recovery workflow and integrated reporting example. The combined saved workflow accepts all eight types in a common row order and can include explicit per-model category, agreement and reliability requests before saving/reopening reports. Use all original retained chains/draws when required by those operations; warnings and unavailable results are part of the output. A complete report means its requested sections executed, not that inference or model selection was accepted.

What the current evidence permits

The current mathematical and computational checks, recovery studies and user workflow examples answer different questions. Their results must not be pooled into one "validated model" label. In particular, the completed normalized-prior recovery assessment concerns a fixed pure-Q, independent two-dimensional design with 50 persons, five items, five raters and four categories. It used 32 independent paired blocks across two truth scenarios and three loading-prior widths. The 192 fits are not 192 independent replications of each condition. That study does not validate all eight result types, arbitrary Q or correlated/3D models.

Research use should state the actual model, prior, prediction target, diagnostics and unresolved limitations. An experimental API is not a recommendation for every study that can supply its inputs.

A bounded foundation, not a universal validation label

The October 14 scope decision retains a defensible experimental research workflow: specify the normalized exchangeable target, compute its posterior, inspect diagnostics and Monte Carlo uncertainty, report its named quantities, and evaluate additional ratings at known facet levels. It does not adopt that prior for every application or certify general interval coverage. Scientific and unfamiliar-user review are still required before foundation acceptance.

The statistical evidence is narrower than the set of inputs the code accepts. The evaluated foundation has two independent dimensions, fixed pure Q (items I1/I2 measure dimension 1; I3/I4/I5 measure dimension 2), positive estimated loadings and rater consistency, item-owned four-category PCM steps and a fixed 1.7 logit multiplier. Its complete data contain 50 people × 5 items × 5 raters. These are study conditions, not recommended minimum sample sizes. The prior anchors location/scale; they are not identified by the likelihood alone.

The analysis holds person/item SD at 1, severity/step kernel SD at √2 and log-consistency kernel SD at √2/2, and compares log-loading SD 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0. R0 uses loadings (0.7, 1.3, 0.7, 1.0, 1.3); R1 changes the first two to (0.35, 0.5). R0 and R1 are generating conditions under the same fitted model, not competing model families. Their prediction difference cannot decide between MFRM and MGMFRM or between one and several dimensions. Higher log loss can also reflect greater inherent outcome uncertainty; prediction error relative to the known truth is a different quantity.

For example, at R1/I1 the 90% coverage counts are 1 confirmed / 0 unresolved for prior SD 0.25, 20 / 3 for SD 0.5 and 26 / 3 for SD 1.0, each out of 32. The wider-prior observed ranges are 62.5–71.875% and 81.25–90.625%; they are not confidence intervals or a calibration certificate. Recovery summaries retain numerical failures and condition on eligible fits where stated. No finite unconditional RMSE guarantee is supplied for failed fits.

For a new application, state the actual model, threshold owner, scale and prior; check data support and the diagnostics of every reported quantity; report unavailable precision and failed attempts. A converged fit cannot establish that the model or prior answers the application question. Correlated/3D models, mixed or sparse Q, fixed slopes, mean profiles, unknown facet levels, joint-prior SBC and design-selection recommendations need their own evidence. Existing operations for some of these cases remain available within their documented experimental scope; the two-dimensional foundation study does not validate them.

What Changes When You Choose MGMFRM

Suppose several raters score responses on criteria intended to measure two abilities. The analysis must separate ability differences from item difficulty and rater severity. A fixed-coefficient multidimensional MFRM assigns each active item-dimension link a coefficient of one. MGMFRM additionally estimates how strongly each active dimension affects an item's ratings and how sharply each rater's category probabilities concentrate. Choose between these models according to that question and the rating design; the additional parameters are assumptions to investigate, not an automatic improvement.

For the independent fixed-Q MGMFRM:

ComponentEstimated or specified?Interpretation
Q pattern and active loadingsThe zero/nonzero pattern is supplied; each active positive loading is estimated.Fixed Q does not mean fixed coefficients. A zero excludes a dimension from that item. Multiple active dimensions enter an additive weighted sum.
Abilities and item difficultiesPerson abilities and item difficulties are estimated; the ability prior mean is zero and person_sd is supplied.The prior SD is not estimated population variability or an individual's posterior uncertainty. Separate ability/item locations depend on the declared priors.
Rater severity and consistencyBoth are estimated subject to zero-sum severity and product-one consistency constraints.Severity shifts the rating distribution. Consistency multiplies the category logits; larger values concentrate probability around the most favored category or tied categories, holding other parameters fixed. It is not a test-retest reliability coefficient or a measure of accuracy against an external standard.
Category stepsFree item-specific steps are estimated; the baseline step is zero and the last nonbaseline step is reconstructed to make their sum zero.Category use can differ by item. The steps for an item are shared across raters and dimensions.
Dependence between dimensionsAbility prior correlation is fixed to identity.This is a population-prior assumption, not a finding that posterior estimates or observed scores are independent. Estimating correlation requires a separate admitted 2D or 3D model; their priors and scopes differ.

The response multiplier is fixed at 1.7 in MGMFRM; fixed-coefficient MFRM uses unit logits. Raw ability or difficulty estimates from the two models therefore cannot be compared as though the scale and prior were unchanged. The equations and experimental guide give the parameter and prior conventions. In particular, the compatibility value discrimination = :none does not disable the estimated loadings in an MGMFRM specification.

Read constraints together with priors. With more than two raters, equal SDs on free coordinates in the raw GeneralizedPrior do not give every constrained rater the same marginal prior. The last sorted rater is reconstructed from the others; changing which rater occupies that position can change the prior and posterior. Likewise, finite posterior intervals for separate ability and item locations do not establish likelihood identification. The location explanation shows which joint shift leaves rating probabilities unchanged and how to inspect its mixing without changing the model. The explicitly normalized exchangeable prior removes the raw rater-label asymmetry by changing the prior target; it does not by itself establish likelihood identification or calibrated inference.

Interpreting Support and Versions

“Supported” describes the documented implementation boundary, not proof of convergence or validity for a new dataset. Experimental availability does not establish recovery, robustness, or external-software agreement. Known-truth simulation and protocol helpers have their own research API reference; their presence does not change the fitting boundary above.

The registered release remains the default installation. Development versions may contain unreleased behavior and should be pinned to an explicit revision when used in reproducible work.

Read the manual and examples for the installed revision. Development documentation can describe features absent from an earlier registered release.

For reporting scope and current limitations of bias, separation/reliability, Strata and score agreement, see the MFRM reporting guide. In particular, the historical posterior separation screen is not a validated FACETS or EAP reliability estimator; it should not be promoted by a Strata transformation.

Scalar temporal severity reports

Experimental Rater Drift checks regular-grid input, fits four RW1 chains and restores them in the original environment. Reports retain reference-relative intervals, MCSE, warnings, figures and portable re-saving. Matched static/RW1 comparison retains model-conditional location uncertainty and structural-zero changes. It does not import historical private chains, compute new contrasts or establish coverage/detection performance. Its rater-owned step model is separate from item-step design planning.
