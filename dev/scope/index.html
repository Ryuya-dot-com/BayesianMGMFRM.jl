Scope and Releases

Development manual

Use the matching source and installation instructions. This is a development snapshot, not an accepted stable release. Model support and scientific acceptance remain as stated on each page.

Choose a model by the structure of your ratings and the quantities you need to estimate. The table lists implemented models, available operations and specific restrictions. Numerical precision and model adequacy must be assessed for your data, regardless of the model family.

Current Support

Three questions require different checks:

  • Can the package perform the requested operation? Check the model, prior, backend and supported combinations below.
  • Is this fitted result adequate for the analysis? Inspect design information, R-hat, ESS, divergences, energy diagnostics, MCSE and predictive checks.
  • What performance has been demonstrated? Recovery and coverage studies apply to their stated generating conditions, priors and designs; see the evidence summary.

The design-planning guide connects preparation, execution, full-roster summaries and saved reproduction. Its fixed-truth and joint-prior SBC examples address declared quantities and precision objectives. They do not supply minimum sample sizes or calibration guarantees for other designs.

The module name BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental and existing saved status fields are retained for API and file compatibility. Interfaces in that namespace may change in a minor release; pin the package version when relying on them. BayesianMGMFRM.Experimental.surface_contract() records its exact current family configurations and constraints. For slope restrictions, inspect surface_contract(model; prior) instead of inheriting a family-level backend list. Unwrapped normalized, slope-restricted and mean-profile models all return NormalizedMGMFRMFit; distinguish their priors and coefficient policies using fit_metadata, not the result type alone. The historical experimental = true keyword is a compatibility route and does not define the forward-looking API.

For their exact parameter meanings and runnable entry points, see exchangeable rater priors, normalized MGMFRM priors and the normalized fitting example. The two uses of "exchangeable" above are different prior types for different models. Neither estimates a population rater SD from the data.

Common operations and model-specific limits

For tested Julia/environment combinations, native-cache restrictions and the steps to reopen or share an analysis, see the saved-result reproduction guide. The declared Julia minimum is not a guarantee for every environment or saved format.

Experimental.JointRatings has distinct model/result contracts. Use the shared load_fit, posterior_summary and save_report verbs for persistence/reporting; its specialized predict and score operations retain their documented targets. Its records are not accepted by the common fit_report/load_fit_cache workflow or the comparison and recovery functions in the table below. A computed response score is not an accepted heldout model comparison.

The candidate connects eight result types to common saved-result operations: BayesianMGMFRM.Legacy.MFRMFit, GMFRMFit, MGMFRMFit, Experimental.NormalizedMGMFRMFit, Experimental.CorrelatedMGMFRMFit, MultidimensionalMFRMFit, Experimental.CorrelatedMFRMFit and Experimental.ExchangeableMFRMFit. An operation being available does not establish that every model is appropriate for the same research question. Unsupported combinations remain unsupported.

The 3D response-surface API specifically accepts independent MGMFRMFit results with GeneralizedPrior and at least two dimensions. It does not accept normalized, slope-restricted, mean-profile or correlated fits, even where their numerical prediction and posterior interval summaries are available. Plot support is separate from fit and common-report support.

Start with the saved comparison and recovery workflow and integrated reporting example. The combined saved workflow accepts all eight types in a common row order and can include explicit per-model category, agreement and reliability requests before saving/reopening reports. Use all original retained chains/draws when required by those operations; warnings and unavailable results are part of the output. A complete report means its requested sections executed, not that inference or model selection was accepted.

Shared-event analysis: available and missing operations

The scalar shared-effect model fits person-by-rater or person-by-testlet effects with mfrm_spec / fit, and through R fit_mgmfrm(..., random_effects = ...). Posterior summaries, contrasts, sampler diagnostics and saved results are available. The following operations share the same posterior draws and preserve numerical warnings:

  • Standard MRCML and shared-event fits support assessment/performance-unit LOO and explicit event-deletion refits. Integrate the shared event effect once over the joint response profile; the standard model uses its joint conditional likelihood.
  • Q3 and Infit/Outfit describe residual fit conditional on fitted effects and provide posterior predictive reference distributions. Finite-cohort posterior reliability and numerical MCSE are available; population/design reliability is not inferred from one fitted cohort.
  • R random_effects composes with group effects, DFF on the finite facets, and fixed calibration of the finite facets. Shared effects themselves cannot simultaneously be threshold owners, fixed anchors, or fixed DFF interactions.
  • Bayesian multivariate G-theory separates score-scale covariance components and supplies posterior D-study coefficients.

For a standard-model check followed by a shared-effect refit, compare predictions for the same held-out unit. Individual-rating LOO and integrated assessment LOO answer different questions and cannot be compared directly. Residual dependence can motivate a shared-effect model, but does not by itself identify a halo mechanism. See shared event effects for model specification and interpretation.

What the current evidence permits

The current mathematical and computational checks, recovery studies and user workflow examples answer different questions. Their results must not be pooled into one "validated model" label. In particular, the completed normalized-prior recovery assessment concerns a fixed pure-Q, independent two-dimensional design with 50 persons, five items, five raters and four categories. It used 32 independent paired blocks across two truth scenarios and three loading-prior widths. The 192 fits are not 192 independent replications of each condition. That study does not validate all eight result types, arbitrary Q or correlated/3D models.

How well are unused ratings predicted?The separate 32-block main assessment is complete: 960 fits, 938 numerically qualified, and 171 of 192 complete five-fold panels eligible. Six conditional mean log losses and three R0→R1 contrasts meet the declared replication and MCMC-error screens.Each fit trains on 1,000 ratings and scores 250 at known person/item/rater levels. All four prior-width contrasts fail the relative-MCSE screen; they cannot select a prior. No new-level or unconditional prediction guarantee follows.
Which prior should be the default?No universal MGMFRM prior default is adopted. Prior-only scale anchoring (NormalizedMGMFRMPrior at SD 0.25) shrinks a low-loading dimension's overall spread toward 1.0 and causes severe undercoverage on R1/I1; Experimental.ScaleIdentifiedMGMFRMPrior separates dimension SD $\sigma_{\theta_d}$ from pure-item relative loadings, but users must still choose :geometric_mean_one or :anchor_item, location identification, and prior scales explicitly.Prior-only SD 0.5 and 1.0 also remain unaccepted as general defaults. Computational defaults are not universal scientific recommendations.
Can a researcher reproduce the workflow?The published development source has an eight-type saved workflow and short actual-fit operability checks, including report/cache reconstruction. The bounded claim and prior-default decision is recorded for this evidence.Short fits do not establish adequate inference. Remaining integrated/environment acceptance, independent scientific review, unfamiliar-user reproduction and release acceptance stay open.

See the saved sensitivity illustration for the observed prior dependence and the results guide for interpreting comparisons. Research use should state the actual model, prior, prediction target, diagnostics and unresolved limitations. An available API is not a recommendation for every study that can supply its inputs.

A bounded foundation, not a universal validation label

The evaluated normalized-prior workflow is: specify the normalized exchangeable target, compute its posterior, inspect diagnostics and Monte Carlo uncertainty, report its named quantities, and evaluate additional ratings at known facet levels. It does not adopt that prior for every application or certify general interval coverage.

The statistical evidence is narrower than the set of inputs the code accepts. The evaluated foundation has two independent dimensions, fixed pure Q (items I1/I2 measure dimension 1; I3/I4/I5 measure dimension 2), positive estimated loadings and rater consistency, item-owned four-category PCM steps and a fixed 1.7 logit multiplier. Its complete data contain 50 people × 5 items × 5 raters. These are study conditions, not recommended minimum sample sizes. The prior anchors location/scale; they are not identified by the likelihood alone.

The analysis holds person/item SD at 1, severity/step kernel SD at √2 and log-consistency kernel SD at √2/2, and compares log-loading SD 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0. R0 uses loadings (0.7, 1.3, 0.7, 1.0, 1.3); R1 changes the first two to (0.35, 0.5). R0 and R1 are generating conditions under the same fitted model, not competing model families. Their prediction difference cannot decide between MFRM and MGMFRM or between one and several dimensions. Higher log loss can also reflect greater inherent outcome uncertainty; prediction error relative to the known truth is a different quantity.

For example, at R1/I1 the 90% coverage counts are 1 confirmed / 0 unresolved for prior SD 0.25, 20 / 3 for SD 0.5 and 26 / 3 for SD 1.0, each out of 32. The wider-prior observed ranges are 62.5–71.875% and 81.25–90.625%; they are not confidence intervals or a calibration certificate. Recovery summaries retain numerical failures and condition on eligible fits where stated. No finite unconditional RMSE guarantee is supplied for failed fits.

For a new application, state the actual model, threshold owner, scale and prior; check data support and the diagnostics of every reported quantity; report unavailable precision and failed attempts. A converged fit cannot establish that the model or prior answers the application question. Correlated/3D models, mixed or sparse Q, fixed slopes, mean profiles, unknown facet levels, joint-prior SBC and design-selection recommendations need their own evidence. Existing operations for some of these cases remain available within their documented configurations; the two-dimensional foundation study does not validate them.

What Changes When You Choose MGMFRM

Suppose several raters score responses on criteria intended to measure two abilities. The analysis must separate ability differences from item difficulty and rater severity. A fixed-coefficient multidimensional MFRM assigns each active item-dimension link a coefficient of one. MGMFRM additionally estimates how strongly each active dimension affects an item's ratings and how sharply each rater's category probabilities concentrate. Choose between these models according to that question and the rating design; the additional parameters are assumptions to investigate, not an automatic improvement.

For the independent fixed-Q MGMFRM:

ComponentEstimated or specified?Interpretation
Q pattern and active loadingsThe zero/nonzero pattern is supplied; each active positive loading is estimated.Fixed Q does not mean fixed coefficients. A zero excludes a dimension from that item. Multiple active dimensions enter an additive weighted sum.
Abilities and item difficultiesPerson abilities and item difficulties are estimated; the ability prior mean is zero and person_sd is supplied.The prior SD is not estimated population variability or an individual's posterior uncertainty. Separate ability/item locations depend on the declared priors.
Rater severity and consistencyBoth are estimated subject to zero-sum severity and product-one consistency constraints.Severity shifts the rating distribution. Consistency multiplies the category logits; larger values concentrate probability around the most favored category or tied categories, holding other parameters fixed. It is not a test-retest reliability coefficient or a measure of accuracy against an external standard.
Category stepsFree item-specific steps are estimated; the baseline step is zero and the last nonbaseline step is reconstructed to make their sum zero.Category use can differ by item. The steps for an item are shared across raters and dimensions.
Dependence between dimensionsAbility prior correlation is fixed to identity.This is a population-prior assumption, not a finding that posterior estimates or observed scores are independent. Estimating correlation requires a separate admitted 2D or 3D model; their priors and scopes differ.

The response multiplier is fixed at 1.7 in MGMFRM; fixed-coefficient MFRM uses unit logits. Raw ability or difficulty estimates from the two models therefore cannot be compared as though the scale and prior were unchanged. The equations and model extensions guide give the parameter and prior conventions. In particular, the compatibility value discrimination = :none does not disable the estimated loadings in an MGMFRM specification.

Read constraints together with priors. With more than two raters, equal SDs on free coordinates in the raw GeneralizedPrior do not give every constrained rater the same marginal prior. The last sorted rater is reconstructed from the others; changing which rater occupies that position can change the prior and posterior. Likewise, finite posterior intervals for separate ability and item locations do not establish likelihood identification. The location explanation shows which joint shift leaves rating probabilities unchanged and how to inspect its mixing without changing the model. The explicitly normalized exchangeable prior removes the raw rater-label asymmetry by changing the prior target; it does not by itself establish likelihood identification or calibrated inference.

Interpreting Support and Versions

Available operations describe what this version can compute. Convergence, model adequacy and precision must be assessed for each analysis. Availability alone does not establish recovery, robustness or external-software agreement. Known-truth simulation and protocol helpers have their own research API reference; their presence does not change the fitting boundary above.

Use the matching source snapshot for this manual. Other installed versions describe their own support; preserve the exact revision/archive for reproducible work.

Read the manual and examples for the installed revision. Development documentation can describe features absent from an earlier registered release.

For reporting scope and current limitations of bias, separation/reliability, Strata and score agreement, see the MFRM reporting guide. In particular, the historical posterior separation screen is not a validated FACETS or EAP reliability estimator; it should not be promoted by a Strata transformation.

Scalar temporal severity reports

Rater Drift checks regular-grid input, fits four RW1 chains and restores them in the original environment. Reports retain reference-relative intervals, MCSE, warnings, figures and portable re-saving. Matched static/RW1 comparison retains model-conditional location uncertainty and structural-zero changes. It does not import historical private chains, compute new contrasts or establish coverage/detection performance. Its rater-owned step model is separate from item-step design planning.

Correct/total responses have an scalar count route: binomial or beta-binomial observations, explicit trials, explanatory population intercepts, known-owner prediction, denominator-aware diagnostics and saved reports. This does not establish word-specific difficulty, random slopes, joint outcomes or new-owner prediction.

Interaction (DFF) Scale Priors, Local Dependence, and Non-Goals

Interaction / DFF prior rationale (explicit choice, no silent default)

Jointly fitted differential facet functioning (interactions in mfrm_spec, such as (:rater, :item) => prior or (:rater, :step) => prior on the doubly centered Kronecker–Helmert subspace) requires an explicit prior scale specification—no silent default is substituted when interactions are declared:

Prior familyRole and recommendationMathematical and computational rationale
HalfNormal(s) (truncated(Normal(0, s), 0, Inf))Recommended zero-supporting interaction SD priorPositive density at $\tau = 0$ permits shrinkage to pure additive main effects when interaction evidence is weak, while Gaussian tails give finite marginal cell and tetrad-contrast variance (implied_tetrad_contrast_sd in design_summary(spec)). Verified against CmdStan 2.39.
Half-t(df = 3–4, s) (truncated(LocationScale(0, s, TDist(df)), 0, Inf))Recommended heavy-bias-tolerant interaction SD priorPositive density at $\tau = 0$ with finite variance ($\nu > 2$), accommodating occasional larger focal rater–item bias spikes better than HalfNormal without inducing pathological tail funnels. Verified against CmdStan 2.39.
LogNormal(mu, sd)Supported explicit positive-scale alternativeUseful when prior domain knowledge bounds $\tau$ strictly away from zero, but has zero density at $\tau = 0$ and can resist full collapse to zero when true DFF is absent.
Half-Cauchy (df = 1)Excluded from recommended presets (design_summary emits a funnel warning for df <= 2)Infinite prior variance ($\mathbb{E}[\tau^2] = \infty$) produces severe HMC funnel geometry and divergent transitions when sparse rater–item cells carry limited information.
Laplace (Bayesian LASSO)Excluded from recommended presetsA fixed Laplace penalty combines weak concentration near zero with exponential tail shrinkage that over-shrinks genuinely large focal biases (Carvalho, Polson, & Scott, 2010).
LKJ(eta) (e.g., LKJ(2))Correlation matrices $R$ onlyApplies strictly to positive-definite correlation matrices in multi-criterion, random-slope, and multidimensional models—never to scalar interaction standard deviations.
Regularized Horseshoe (Piironen & Vehtari, 2017)Not implementedStandard coordinate-wise local scales $\lambda_{ri}$ break exchangeability or distort marginal slab widths when projected through doubly centered row- and column-sum-to-zero constraints; deferred pending a subspace-invariant formulation.

Use diagnostics(fit; kind = :pair_bias, pair = (:rater, :item)) (or BayesianMGMFRM.dff_report(fit)) to inspect observed vs. expected scores, score residuals, information-weighted approximate severity-bias logits, and tail probabilities. Its analysis_mode explicitly distinguishes exploratory post-hoc residual screens on main-effects models (:secondary_residual_screen) from posterior interaction summaries on jointly fitted interaction models (:joint_interaction_model). Use diagnostics(fit; kind = :fair_average, by = :rater) to translate logit severities into expected rating-scale scores at sample-mean or zero-origin baselines with full posterior credible intervals and MCSE.

Temporal drift and local-dependence status

Temporal severity drift (Experimental.RaterDrift RW1) and all local-dependence extensions—person-by-testlet cluster residuals (Person-by-Testlet Dependence), paired and star-factorized multi-criterion event association (Joint Ratings, Ordinal Association, Star Association), and rater criterion-collapse mixtures (Criterion Collapse)—remain available through their model-specific interfaces in 0.2.0-DEV.
