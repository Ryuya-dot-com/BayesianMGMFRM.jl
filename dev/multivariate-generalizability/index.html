Univariate and multivariate generalizability theory

Use multivariate G-theory when the question concerns the reliability of several numeric scores, their weighted composite, and the number of raters or tasks needed for a future assessment. The model partitions both variances and covariances by source and carries uncertainty into a D study. One score column specifies univariate G-theory. Choose explicit ML/REML estimation or Bayesian NUTS.

For a person × rater × task design, the score vector is

\[\mathbf y_{prt}=\boldsymbol\mu+\mathbf p_p+\mathbf r_r+\mathbf t_t+ \mathbf{pr}_{pr}+\mathbf{pt}_{pt}+\mathbf{rt}_{rt}+\boldsymbol\epsilon_{prt}.\]

Each effect has its own multivariate normal distribution and covariance matrix. The residual includes the highest interaction and measurement error; one score vector per cell cannot separate those two sources. For one random facet, the components are person, facet, and their interaction/residual. For a nested design, use nesting=(rater=:task,): components are person, task, rater within task, person × task, and person × rater within task/residual. Child IDs can repeat across parents because their joint identities are retained.

These covariance structures follow the common-design formulations in Jiang and Skorupski (2018), especially Equations 8–9. Their BUGS tutorial uses Wishart-based covariance priors. This implementation instead explicitly separates marginal SDs from LKJ correlations, as described in the Stan multivariate hierarchical prior guide. It therefore implements the same covariance decomposition with a different, user-declared prior specification, rather than reproducing the original posterior.

ML and REML

using BayesianMGMFRM
const GT = BayesianMGMFRM.Generalizability
spec = GT.model(scores; person=:Person,
    facets=(rater=:Rater, task=:Task),
    scores=(:Content, :Organization), prior=:none)
fit_gt = fit(spec; method=:reml)   # :ml also available
GT.component_summary(fit_gt)
diagnostics(fit_gt)
d_study = reliability(fit_gt; counts=(rater=3, task=2), weights=[0.6, 0.4])

Use scores=(:Content,) for a single score and nesting=(rater=:task,) for raters nested within tasks. These are Gaussian score models; ordinal Rasch :jmle and :mmle are separate estimators. ML/REML requires prior=:none; it never discards a supplied prior implicitly.

Random effects are integrated analytically. ML profiles one unrestricted mean per score; REML additionally accounts for estimating those means, using the restricted Gaussian likelihood. The parameterization permits positive semidefinite random-effect covariance matrices and requires positive definite residual covariance. The likelihood follows Bates et al. (2015), Section 3.4. Numerical stationarity checks include both factor gradients and covariance boundary conditions; they do not certify a global optimum or adequate sample size.

GT.component_summary() and reliability() report point estimates, standard errors and pointwise asymptotic confidence intervals from observed information and the delta method. Intervals are withheld when a covariance component is on a boundary, optimization checks fail or information is unsuitable. They are not clipped to the parameter bounds. Finite-sample coverage is not established; a narrow interval is not evidence of model adequacy. ML/REML reports do not contain posterior probabilities, R-hat, ESS or MCMC diagnostics. The current implementation uses dense Gaussian covariance algebra; memory and factorization costs grow with the total number of observed scalar scores.

Finite-sample intervals for a complete one-facet design

For a separate table rater_scores with one row per person/rater cell:

one_facet = GT.model(rater_scores; person=:Person, facets=(rater=:Rater,),
    scores=(:Content, :Organization), prior=:none)
fit_one = fit(one_facet; method=:reml)
d = reliability(fit_one; counts=(rater=4,), weights=[0.6, 0.4],
    interval_method=:exact)

This option requires a complete person × one random facet allocation with at least two levels of each. Under the independent Gaussian random-effects model, it uses the F pivot for G and the chi-square pivot for relative SEM. Weights must be specified independently of the observed scores. Multivariate mean-square matrices retain cross-score covariance, so the same calculation applies to each score or a prespecified weighted composite. The usual consistency-ICC F interval is documented in the Stata ICC manual, Methods and formulas, pp. 20–22. Changing the future replication count is a monotone transformation of that variance-ratio interval.

ML/REML point estimates are retained; these exact intervals come from the raw Gaussian mean squares, not the fitted covariance Hessian. They can remain available when a fitted covariance component is on a boundary. The F confidence set is intersected with the nonnegative person-variance model; if that intersection is empty, bounds remain missing with status=:empty_confidence_set. It is not replaced by a zero-width interval at zero. These are pointwise confidence sets, not posterior probabilities or simultaneous bands.

This option does not supply intervals for Phi, absolute SEM or the other variance summaries (status=:not_supported_by_exact); it never silently falls back to delta intervals. Incomplete or two-facet designs are rejected. New ML/REML reports store the necessary mean squares. Older reports without them retain delta calculations but cannot supply exact intervals without their original complete score data.

Joint uncertainty across future designs

For a complete balanced one- or two-facet design, including raters nested within tasks, interval_method=:mean_square_region supplies intervals for G, Phi, both SEM metrics and the two variance summaries:

region_d = reliability(fit_gt; counts=(rater=4, task=2), weights=[0.6, 0.4],
    interval_method=:mean_square_region)
using CairoMakie
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(fit_gt; kind=:dstudy, metric=:Phi,
    counts=(rater=1:6, task=1:3), weights=[0.6, 0.4],
    interval_method=:mean_square_region)

The source must contain every person/facet cell, with equal child counts per parent in a nested design. Independent normal random effects give independent chi-square pivots for the ANOVA mean squares. The implementation forms their simultaneous region, intersects it with nonnegative variance components, and projects it onto the requested coefficients and error variances. The SEM intervals are square roots of the error-variance limits. These are extensions of the same chi-square variance pivot used in the one-facet calculation.

Under those assumptions, the projections have at least the requested coverage jointly across future counts and metrics for one fixed score or prespecified composite. This does not provide joint coverage across the different scores or data-selected weights. Intervals can be substantially wider than pointwise or asymptotic intervals; more planned raters do not add information to the original G-study data. They are not posterior probabilities, profile likelihood intervals or distribution-robust intervals.

ML/REML points and numerical warnings are retained. A covariance boundary does not prevent the mean-square calculation. Empty regions, degenerate mean squares and unresolved numerical ranges retain explicit statuses and missing bounds. Incomplete designs and older reports lacking the ANOVA summaries are rejected for this method. No automatic method substitution occurs. New fits save the small mean-square matrices and degrees of freedom, so subsequent calculations and plots do not fit another model or draw random samples.

Bayesian estimation

Supply a wide table with one row per person/facet cell and one numeric column per criterion. All declared criteria must be observed in every supplied row. Entire missing cells are allowed when the covariance components remain distinguishable; duplicate cells and aliased covariance components are rejected. There are one or two random measurement facets. Criteria are the fixed vector of outcomes, not another randomly sampled facet.

using BayesianMGMFRM
using Distributions: Normal, truncated, LKJ
const GT = BayesianMGMFRM.Generalizability

spec = GT.model(scores;
    person=:Person, facets=(rater=:Rater, task=:Task),
    scores=(:Content, :Organization),
    prior=(mean=Normal(0, 5),
           sd=truncated(Normal(0, 2), 0, Inf),
           correlation=LKJ(2, 2)))
validate_design(spec)
fit_gt = fit(spec; chains=4, warmup=1000, ndraws=1000, seed=42)
posterior_summary(fit_gt)
posterior_mcse(fit_gt)
diagnostics(fit_gt)
qualify(fit_gt)
predictive_check(fit_gt; seed=43)

For a single score, supply correlation=nothing instead of an LKJ prior. Choose mean and SD prior scales in the actual score units; the numbers above are illustrative. The covariance prior is Σ = Diagonal(sd) * R * Diagonal(sd), with independent HalfNormal SDs and an LKJ correlation matrix for each component. Effects use noncentered standard normal coordinates; no sample-centering constraint changes their population distribution. prior_draw(spec; seed=...) and simulate(spec, draw; seed=...) generate a complete score table on the declared allocation.

The Gaussian observation model treats the scores as numeric measurements. For coarse ordinal categories, floor/ceiling concentrations or strongly non-Gaussian scores, inspect replicated means, variances and cross-score covariances and consider an ordinal MGMFRM analysis. Passing a structural screen does not establish adequate information or model fit. R-hat 1.01, bulk/tail ESS 400, energy and sampler warnings remain computational reference criteria; required MCSE depends on the quantity and decision.

Bayesian D-study coefficients and probabilities

d_study = reliability(fit_gt;
    counts=(rater=3, task=2), weights=[0.6, 0.4],
    threshold=0.80, interval=0.95)

For each criterion and the composite, the result includes posterior means and credible intervals for relative G, absolute Φ, relative/absolute SEM, universe variance and absolute error variance. It also gives P(G ≥ 0.80 | data) and P(Φ ≥ 0.80 | data), with chain-aware MCSE. Constant sampled indicators have unresolved probability precision; an empirical proportion of zero or one is not reported as a precisely established probability. Whole-fit diagnostic warnings remain attached to every D-study result.

For a crossed design with counts n_r and n_t,

\[\Sigma_\delta=\Sigma_{pr}/n_r+\Sigma_{pt}/n_t+\Sigma_e/(n_r n_t),\qquad \Sigma_\Delta=\Sigma_\delta+\Sigma_r/n_r+\Sigma_t/n_t+\Sigma_{rt}/(n_r n_t).\]

For each posterior draw and weight vector w,

\[G=\frac{w^\mathsf T\Sigma_p w}{w^\mathsf T(\Sigma_p+\Sigma_\delta)w},\qquad \Phi=\frac{w^\mathsf T\Sigma_p w}{w^\mathsf T(\Sigma_p+\Sigma_\Delta)w}.\]

Counts always describe a future complete balanced allocation of new random facet levels. A nested child count is per parent, so child and residual contributions are divided by the parent count times the child count. Counts are required explicitly, even for an unbalanced G study. Weights keep their supplied scale: doubling them preserves G/Φ and doubles SEM. Omitting weights uses the equal-score mean. These are population design coefficients, distinct from the finite-cohort reliability of posterior person estimates in an ordinal model.

Interpret covariance alongside criterion discrimination

A person covariance describes stable relationships among criteria. Rater covariance reflects criterion tendencies shared by a rater; person × rater covariance describes a rater's person-specific tendencies recurring across tasks. The residual covariance captures remaining dependence within a complete person/rater/task cell. Positive covariance in any one component does not by itself establish psychological halo. With only one facet, person × rater and measurement residual covariance are combined.

Criterion discrimination in MGMFRM acts on the latent measurement scale. Multivariate G-theory instead decomposes numeric score covariance; unequal discrimination can change the signal/error pattern, but is not separately identified by adding free loadings to an unrestricted person covariance. Use the models to answer complementary questions: MGMFRM for criterion loadings and ordinal response behavior, G-theory for where score covariance occurs and how replication affects reliability. Raw score correlations and latent correlations are not interchangeable, and neither a large correlation nor improved model fit alone establishes a causal halo mechanism.

Save joint results

save_fit("gtheory.jls", fit_gt)  # trusted cache for the same source/environment
receipt = save_report("gtheory.json", fit_gt; return_receipt=true)
report = load_report(receipt.path; expected_sha256=receipt.sha256)
new_plan = reliability(report; counts=(rater=4, task=2), weights=[0.6, 0.4])

The Bayesian portable report retains joint covariance draws, criterion/component identities, chain and iteration IDs, priors, diagnostic warnings and producer hashes. The ML/REML report retains covariance estimates, the joint information-based uncertainty when available, balanced ANOVA mean squares when applicable, identities, optimization diagnostics and producer hashes. Reopening either report or changing D-study counts does not rerun estimation. Keep the SHA-256 receipt separately. The R wrapper supports Gaussian ML/REML through fit_mgmfrm(data, model="gtheory", method="reml", score=c("Content", "Organization"), facets=c(rater="Rater", task="Task"), counts=list(rater=1:6, task=c(1L,2L))). One score name requests a univariate analysis. plot(result, type="variance"), type="covariance" and type="dstudy" draw stored estimates and confidence intervals without Julia. vcov(result) is the approximate sampling covariance of the component estimates. Preserve the complete R object with saveRDS(). Bayesian G-theory is specified through Julia.

After fitting the corresponding one-facet model in R, new designs can be calculated from its saved object without Julia:

gt_one <- fit_mgmfrm(rater_scores, model="gtheory", method="reml",
                    person="Person", score=c("Content", "Organization"),
                    facets=c(rater="Rater"))
saveRDS(gt_one, "one-facet.rds")
# In a later R session, these operations do not start Julia.
gt_one <- readRDS("one-facet.rds")
d <- diagnostics_mgmfrm(gt_one, "reliability", counts=list(rater=1:8),
                       weights=c(0.6, 0.4), interval_method="exact")
plot(gt_one, type="dstudy", metric="G", counts=list(rater=1:8),
     weights=c(0.6, 0.4), interval_method="exact")

Use interval_method="delta" for the existing approximation, including two-facet plans. Nested child counts remain per parent. The covariance and optimization record are preserved when counts, weights or the confidence level change. A design grid need not have been supplied at fit time.

For a saved complete balanced two-facet analysis, use:

diagnostics_mgmfrm(gt, "reliability",
  counts=list(rater=1:6, task=1:3), weights=c(0.6, 0.4),
  interval_method="mean_square_region")
plot(gt, type="dstudy", metric="Phi",
  counts=list(rater=1:6, task=1:3), weights=c(0.6, 0.4),
  interval_method="mean_square_region")

R uses the same region definition offline and reuses each fixed-score region across the displayed plans. Figure labels identify the statistical interval method; the numerical fit qualification remains visible when it needs review.

Visualize saved results

using CairoMakie
fig = BayesianMGMFRM.plot(fit_gt; kind=:variance)
save("gstudy-variance.svg", fig)
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(fit_gt; kind=:covariance)
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(fit_gt; kind=:dstudy,
    counts=(rater=1:6, task=[1,2]), score="composite", metric=:Phi,
    weights=[0.6, 0.4])

The same plots accept SHA-verified saved reports. Components retain signed covariances. D-study curves show each declared allocation and pointwise intervals, with no new fitting or random sampling. Figure captions distinguish confidence intervals from credible intervals and retain numerical qualification. By default the full caption is written to annotation_io=stdout; use an IO stream to save it alongside the image, or annotation_io=nothing to embed it. The organization follows mfrmr's component and D-study views; statistical intervals and warnings refer to the estimator used here. For a complete one-facet ML/REML fit, kind=:dstudy, metric=:G or :relative_sem also accepts interval_method=:exact. Empty confidence sets are identified in the caption and omitted from the interval bars.

BayesianMGMFRM.Generalizability.model — Function
Generalizability.model(table; scores, person, facets, nesting=(;), prior)

Declare numeric score columns and one or two random measurement facets, e.g. facets=(rater=:Rater,task=:Task). A nested design uses nesting=(rater=:task,); child IDs are local to their parent. Use prior=:none with fit(...; method=:ml) or :reml, or supply explicit prior=(mean=Normal(...), sd=truncated(Normal(0,s),0,Inf), correlation=LKJ(length(scores),eta)) for NUTS. A single score uses correlation=nothing. All criteria must be observed in each row. Duplicate cells must be resolved explicitly. The highest interaction and measurement residual form one component.
