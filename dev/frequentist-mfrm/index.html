Frequentist 1D JMLE and MMLE

When you want fast point calibration, classical FACETS/ConQuest/mfrmr/mfrmpy tables, or a direct frequentist baseline alongside a Bayesian posterior on the exact same rating specification, pass method = :jmle or method = :mmle to fit.

Non-Bayesian estimation is intentionally scoped to one-dimensional unit-slope Rating Scale (RSM) and Partial Credit (PCM) models across both standard three-facet BayesianMGMFRM.Legacy.FacetSpec (person, rater, item) and general user-named multi-facet mfrm_spec designs. Multidimensional, free-slope (GPCM), predictor-regression, and random-interaction models remain Bayesian routes.

Quick start: 3-facet and general multi-facet specifications

For a three-facet table (person, rater, item), reuse FacetData and mfrm_spec directly:

using BayesianMGMFRM
using BayesianMGMFRM.GlassBox
using BayesianMGMFRM.Legacy

data = FacetData(ratings; person=:person, rater=:rater, item=:item,
    score=:score, category_levels=0:4)
spec = mfrm_spec(data; thresholds=:rating_scale) # or :partial_credit

fit_jmle = fit(spec; method=:jmle)
fit_mmle = fit(spec; method=:mmle, quad_points=31, population_sd=1.0)

For a general $F$-facet table (for example Person × Rater × Task × Criterion), pass prior = :none to mfrm_spec when only frequentist estimation is needed, or supply prior_sd when you want to run both frequentist and Bayesian fits on the identical specification object:

spec_4f = mfrm_spec(writing;
    person=:Person,
    facets=(Rater=:Rater, Task=:Task, Criterion=:Criterion),
    score=:Score,
    steps=:global,          # or steps=:Criterion for PCM
    category_levels=0:5,
    prior=:none,
)

jml_rsm = fit(spec_4f; method=:jmle)
mml_rsm = fit(spec_4f; method=:mmle, quad_points=31, population_sd=1.0)

Transfer recording locations and category steps to a second panel

To estimate a second panel's mean on an earlier calibration scale, match each recording by ID and each adjacent-category step by its upper score label. Positive recording and step values lower the probability of a higher score. The following three-category example treats the input values as known.

wave2 = (listener=repeat(1:3, inner=2), recording=repeat(["a","b"], 3),
         rating=[1,2,2,2,3,2])
calibration = (facets=(recording=Dict("a"=>0.4, "b"=>1.1),),
               steps=(owner=:global, values=Dict(:global=>Dict(2=>-0.3, 3=>0.3))))
setup = (person=:listener, facets=(recording=:recording,), score=:rating,
         category_levels=1:3, steps=:global, fixed_calibration=calibration)
spec = mfrm_spec(wave2; setup..., prior=:none)
result = fit(spec; method=:mmle, estimate_mean=true, population_sd=1.0)
s = design_summary(result)
(s.population_mean, s.population_mean_se, s.fixed_calibration.uncertainty)

A positive mean places the second panel above the original zero-mean panel on the declared unit-logit scale. Here the conditional standard error treats both the recording locations and the common steps as fixed. Changing the reference SD or transferring estimates with sampling uncertainty changes the question. The alternating pattern of recordings in this example is incidental. Input row and dictionary order can change without changing the identified anchors.

Use steps=(owner=:recording, values=Dict("a"=>Dict(2=>-0.3, 3=>0.3))) inside the calibration to fix recording-specific PCM steps when the specification uses steps=:recording. Each owner's steps sum to zero across its declared categories. When all but one are fixed, the last one is determined by that constraint. An anchored facet keeps absolute locations; any remaining free levels on it use individual location coordinates. Other unanchored ordinary facets retain their sum-zero coding. For an existing scalar BayesianMGMFRM.Legacy.FacetSpec, use mfrm_spec(existing_spec; fixed_calibration=calibration, prior=:none) with its own facet names, such as :item and :rater.

For a Bayesian population-mean contrast, use the same setup and supply an intercept prior on the linked scale. For unit-normal listener deviations and a Normal(0,1) prior on the shared panel mean, use

using Distributions: Normal
bayes = mfrm_spec(wave2; setup..., prior_sd=(person=1.0, facets=(recording=0.0,), step=0.0),
                  intercept=Normal(0,1))
draws = fit(bayes; method=:nuts, chains=4, warmup=400, ndraws=800, seed=4361)
posterior_summary(draws; parameters=["population_mean"])

The zero SDs identify fully fixed structural blocks. For a partially anchored facet the declared SD applies to independent free absolute locations. A partially fixed centered step block of length H retains the original H/(H−1) variance multiplier on orthonormal contrasts of the remaining steps. Numerical intervals from either route condition on the supplied values. A shared shift of the anchors would also shift the recovered panel mean.

Link panels through a subset mean

Suppose recordings "a" and "b" have preliminary locations 0.4 and 1.1 logits. If their equal-weight mean of 0.75 is a defensible reference, allow their individual locations to change in the receiving panel.

subset = (groups=[(block=:recording, levels=["a","b"], mean=0.75)],
          steps=calibration.steps)
linked = mfrm_spec(wave2; setup..., fixed_calibration=subset, prior=:none)
group_fit = fit(linked; method=:mmle, estimate_mean=true, population_sd=1.0)
group_result = design_summary(group_fit)
(group_result.population_mean, group_result.population_mean_se,
 group_result.fixed_calibration.dimensions, facet_summary(group_fit))

The two estimated locations must average 0.75. Each member has a standard error, and their covariance makes the variance of their fixed mean zero. Use the observed-information se for original-scale locations. The separate diagonal conditional_se depends on the free-coordinate coding. The panel SE conditions on the declared mean and fixed steps. If every reference recording has moved by the same amount, these data cannot distinguish that shift from a panel location difference. With different recordings in the reference, compare the resulting panel estimates on their explicitly declared scales.

Use sum=1.5 for the equivalent total in this example. Groups can overlap on a facet, and fixed individual locations can be supplied alongside groups. Consistent redundant equations count once toward the rank. The design summary gives the independent constraint count and remaining facet degrees of freedom. Each level has equal weight regardless of its observation count.

For a Bayesian fit set prior_sd=(person=1., facets=(recording=1.,), step=0.) and intercept=Normal(0,1) on the same specification. On a grouped facet the prior is an isotropic Gaussian restricted to the declared equations. Its center is the feasible vector with smallest Euclidean norm. The facet SD scales a unit-length contrast in the free subspace. For this pair the SD of the difference between locations is √2 times the specified SD. Unconstrained recordings have prior mean zero. A LogNormal hyperprior places uncertainty on the contrast SD. Compare plausible prior scales, especially for sparsely rated recordings.

Carry preliminary calibration uncertainty into the panel comparison

Suppose the recording and step estimates above came from an independent sample. Supply their covariance in the order of the source coordinates. The receiving model matches recording IDs and the upper scores of adjacent steps. A three-category rating scale has two centered steps and one free step.

coordinates1 = [(block=:recording, level="b", estimate=1.1),
                (block=:steps, owner=:global, level=:global, upper_category=2, estimate=-0.3),
                (block=:recording, level="a", estimate=0.4)]
transfer = (coordinates=coordinates1, covariance=[0.12 -0.01 0.03; -0.01 0.05 0.02; 0.03 0.02 0.08])
linked = (person=:listener, facets=(recording=:recording,), score=:rating,
          category_levels=1:3, calibration=transfer)
res = fit(mfrm_spec(wave2; linked..., prior=:none); method=:mmle, estimate_mean=true, population_sd=1.0)
summary2 = design_summary(res)
(summary2.population_mean, summary2.calibration.conditional_se, summary2.population_mean_se)

The last value includes preliminary sampling uncertainty by the delta method. The panel mean itself is the ordinary plug-in estimate. Other reported MMLE SEs condition on the preliminary estimates. When the receiving information is unqualified, the propagated SE is missing. Both panels must use the same scale, rating direction, step convention and invariant linking parameters. A common displacement of the anchors is confounded with the panel difference.

For a qualified preliminary MRCML MMLE fit, anchor_linking_summary(first_fit; coordinates=mapping) extracts the joint estimates and covariance directly. Each mapping row supplies parameter (the exact name in the preliminary fit), the receiving block and level, and, for a step, owner and upper_category. For example, a source parameter named "Cat1" may map to (parameter="Cat1", block=:steps, owner=:global, level=:global, upper_category=2). Select independent coordinates. Within each step owner, omit at least one transition so the centered constraint can determine the remaining step mean. Preserve the source scale or transform its means and covariance together.

For Bayesian transfer, supply a Gaussian approximation to the preliminary posterior, then use only the receiving observations. For the small example, mfrm_spec(wave2; linked..., prior_sd=(person=1.0, facets=(recording=0.0,), step=0.0), intercept=Normal(0,1)) uses the external covariance for the transferred coordinates and a separate prior on the receiving panel mean. The zero SDs indicate that these blocks have no additional free contrasts. The named population_mean is the panel contrast, and the person coordinates are deviations about that mean. Receiving data can update the transferred coordinates under the joint model. An MLE and its inverse information alone approximate a likelihood. Incorporate the intended calibration prior once when comparing with a concurrent Bayesian analysis. This Gaussian approximation can give a different answer from fitting the original two likelihoods jointly.

Compare an individual to a common cutoff

For the Bayesian receiving-panel fit draws above, choose the location to which the cutoff applies. Here zero represents the reference panel's population mean. The chosen 0.95 probability is an illustrative decision rule.

linked_people = classification(draws; location=:linked, cutoff=0.0,
                               pass_probability=0.95, interval=0.95)
[(r.parameter, r.lower, r.upper, r.qualifying_probability,
  r.qualifying_mcse.value) for r in linked_people.rows]

Each interval and probability uses the person's deviation plus the receiving panel mean in the same draw. The default location=:parameter uses the deviation alone and answers a question about the receiving population's mean. For a within-panel ordering, ranking(draws; direction=:higher, top_k=2) gives the same ranks on either scale because the shift is shared. Different recording calibrations can change individual contrasts. A fixed calibration conditions on the supplied values. An uncertain calibration propagates its joint posterior variation. A sampled probability of zero or one with missing MCSE calls for reporting the event count. Interpret the direction from the rating scale and the role of the person in the design.

Fix one rater-by-task interaction cell

Suppose an external calibration puts judge A's additional severity on task 1 at 0.6 logits after accounting for judge and task main effects. To use this value, declare the interaction and identify the cell by both facet names and their original level IDs. The example has three judges and three tasks.

using BayesianMGMFRM.Legacy: fit_report
crossed = (person=repeat(1:12, inner=9),
           judge=repeat(repeat(["A","B","C"], inner=3), 12),
           task=repeat(1:3, 36),
           rating=[mod(p + j, 3) for p in 1:12 for j in 1:9])
cell_anchor = (levels=(judge="A", task=1), value=0.6)
cell_spec = mfrm_spec(crossed; person=:person, score=:rating,
    facets=(judge=:judge, task=:task), steps=:global, category_levels=0:2,
    interactions=((:judge,:task),), fixed_calibration=(cells=[cell_anchor],),
    prior=:none)
cell_fit = fit(cell_spec; method=:mmle, population_sd=1.0)
effects = filter(r -> startswith(r.parameter, "interaction["),
                 fit_report(cell_fit).facet_measures)
[(r.parameter, r.estimate, r.se, r.fixed) for r in effects]

Positive additional severity lowers each adjacent-category logit by 0.6 at that cell. The interaction sums to zero across each row and column. The two remaining entries in judge A's row therefore sum to −0.6, as do the two remaining entries in task 1's column. Both entries in either pair can still vary. One independent anchor reduces the four free interaction contrasts to three. See design_summary(cell_fit).fixed_calibration.interaction_dimensions for the rank and remaining degrees of freedom. Standard errors condition on the supplied value. Cells determined by the margins also appear as fixed. Several distinct cells can be supplied, provided their values agree with the margins. A zero-sum interaction alone supplies no origin for a panel mean.

For a Bayesian fit, supply an interaction SD in prior_sd.interactions, e.g. (judge_task=0.9,), alongside the usual person, facet and step priors. It defines the cell SD on the original doubly centered grid. At a given scale the Gaussian prior on the remaining contrasts is the one obtained by conditioning on the declared cell values. Its center shifts and its remaining cell variances can differ. A LogNormal prior applies to the scale of this restricted model; fixed values do not supply a likelihood for that scale. Set the interaction SD to zero when the constraints determine all cells. The same cell syntax works with method=:jmle and with scalar mfrm_spec(existing_facet_spec; interactions=..., fixed_calibration=..., prior=:none). Cell declarations currently target facet-by-facet residuals.

Read a two-wave linking report

Use the observed rows from the first wave and the fitted receiving model. The first table must contain only the scored observations used in the preliminary analysis. Set person and level to its column names and block to the receiving facet role. Match the origin, unit and score direction of the preliminary locations to the fitted scale. An ID match alone cannot verify this. The example uses the two-recording group fit above.

wave1 = (listener=["P1","P1","P2","P2"], recording=["a","b","a","b"])
source_locations = Dict("a"=>0.4, "b"=>1.1)
link = anchor_linking_summary(wave1, group_fit; block=:recording,
                              person=:listener, reference=source_locations)
(link.connectivity.shared_levels, link.connectivity.combined_components,
 only(link.groups).member_free_df, link.panel)
[(r.level, r.preliminary_n, r.receiving_n, r.role, r.displacement)
 for r in link.common_parameters]

Here both recordings connect the waves. Their fixed mean leaves one free contrast. Each row gives the receiving location and, where estimable, the receiving minus preliminary change. In this difficulty coding, a positive recording change lowers the expected score at the same listener location. The tables retain first-only and receiving-only levels in connectivity.rows. Components describe the person-by-facet observation graph. Equal person IDs in different waves remain separate. Shared IDs alone cannot establish invariance or the identification of a larger model.

Read the displacement interval with its reference. For a free level or a member of a fixed-mean group it conditions on the supplied preliminary point and uses the receiving fit's observed-information SE or posterior quantiles. The Bayesian rows also give mean and endpoint Monte Carlo errors. An omitted preliminary value leaves the change missing. A fixed location supplies no separately estimable change. A Gaussian calibration updates a common location in the Bayesian fit; this update alone cannot identify a between-wave change. Inspect qualified, warnings, and panel_conditional_se when comparing models. JMLE has no estimated population mean. A common shift of the reference set is inseparable from a panel change without an independent reference.

The report identifies the fitted step owner. For each owner level, centered_step_df gives the degrees of freedom after centering; conditional_free_df gives the number remaining with the transferred coordinates held fixed. transferred_df counts the independent external coordinates. The same report can be saved and reopened with its checksum using receipt=save_report(path, link; return_receipt=true) and load_report(path; expected_sha256=receipt.sha256).

Alignment with FACETS, ConQuest, mfrmr, and mfrmpy

All conventions are explicit so results match across packages when the same estimator and constraints are selected:

  1. Additive logit orientation: By default, person ability enters with +1 and every non-person facet enters with -1 (difficulty / severity):

    \[\log\frac{P(Y_n = k)}{P(Y_n = k - 1)} = \theta_{p(n)} - \sum_{f=1}^{F} \beta_{f, j_f(n)} - \tau_{g(n), k}.\]

    Pass positive_facets = [:Rater] if a facet uses easiness/leniency orientation.
  2. Identification gauge (centering and noncenter_facet):
    • centering = :sum_to_zero (default) constrains free levels of each centered facet to sum to zero ($\sum_\ell \beta_{f, \ell} = 0$) and step thresholds within each step owner to sum to zero ($\sum_s \tau_{g, s} = 0$), matching mfrmr, mfrmpy, FACETS, and ConQuest (lconstraints=items).
    • centering = :reference_first fixes the first level of each centered facet to zero, matching the default gauge of BayesianMGMFRM's BayesianMGMFRM.Legacy.FacetDesign.
    • In JMLE, noncenter_facet = :person (default) leaves interior person abilities unconstrained while centering non-person facets. Individual hard anchors (anchors = (rater = Dict("R1" => 0.0), ...)) fix the anchored levels directly.
  3. MMLE population distribution (population_sd, estimate_sd, estimate_mean):
    • estimate_sd = false, estimate_mean = false (default, population_sd = 1.0): integrates $\theta_p \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_0^2)$ over Golub–Welsch Gauss–Hermite quadrature (quadrature = :gauss_hermite, quad_points = 31 default, or quadrature = :rectangular), matching fixed-SD MML in mfrmr and mfrmpy.
    • estimate_sd = true, estimate_mean = false: estimates $\theta_p \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ jointly with sum-to-zero non-person facets, matching ConQuest set lconstraints=items; estimate ! method=gauss.
    • estimate_sd = true, estimate_mean = true: estimates both mean and SD $\theta_p \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with sum-to-zero non-person facets, matching mfrmr population_formula = ~ 1.
  4. Extreme-score persons in JMLE (extreme_adjustment):
    • extreme_adjustment = 0.0 (default): exact unadjusted JMLE. Persons with all-minimum or all-maximum scores have no finite unpenalized MLE ($\mp\infty$), contribute zero gradient at the limit, and are reported with finite_mle = false and estimate = missing alongside finite Warm WLE scores (wle, wle_se).
    • extreme_adjustment = 0.3: applies the FACETS EXTRIM = 0.3 score adjustment to extreme person totals.

Standard errors, person scores, and fit statistics

Frequentist uncertainty quantities are not Bayesian posterior credible intervals or MCMC Monte Carlo standard errors. Reports keep three uncertainty quantities distinct:

  • se (:constrained_observed_information): Exact delta-method standard error from the inverse observed Fisher information on the identified free coordinates (J * inv(H_free) * J'), computed via exact block Schur complement in unadjusted JMLE and exact marginal-log-likelihood Hessian in MMLE.
  • conditional_se (ModelSE in JMLE): FACETS-style diagonal conditional approximation 1 / sqrt(sum(w_r * Var(Y_r | eta_hat_r))), holding all other parameters fixed.
  • model_se & real_se (RealSE): model_se equals se in MMLE and conditional_se in JMLE (matching mfrmr and mfrmpy), with misfit-inflated real_se = model_se * sqrt(max(1.0, infit)).

Person scores (facet_summary(fit; facet = :person) or fit_report(fit).person_scores) provide:

  • In MMLE: eap, eap_sd (quadrature posterior SD conditional on point calibration; calibration uncertainty is not propagated), eap_q05/eap_q50/eap_q95, Warm's bias-reduced wle and wle_se, and fixed-calibration mle / mle_se.
  • In JMLE: joint estimate, joint Hessian se, conditional_se, and fixed-calibration wle / wle_se.

Fit diagnostics (fit_stats(fit), facets_report(fit)) report Infit and Outfit mean squares together with Wright & Masters (1982, Eq. 4.27) FACETS degrees of freedom and Wilson–Hilferty cube-root ZSTD (WHEXACT = Yes by default via whexact = true, evaluated for all df > 0 with optional zstd_cap = 9.0), alongside homogeneous-variance infit_zstd_engine and outfit_zstd_engine columns and both composite (ptmea) and person-only (ptmea_person) point-measure correlations.

Inspecting, comparing, saving, and plotting

using BayesianMGMFRM.Legacy: fit_report
qualify(mml_rsm)                        # gradient, positive-definite Hessian & boundary check
rep = fit_report(mml_rsm)               # portable summary, facet/step/person tables, reliability
contrast(mml_rsm; facet=:Rater, left="R1", right="R4")

# Compare RSM vs. PCM under MMLE (AIC / BIC / likelihood-ratio summary):
spec_pcm = mfrm_spec(writing; person=:Person,
    facets=(Rater=:Rater, Task=:Task, Criterion=:Criterion),
    score=:Score, steps=:Criterion, category_levels=0:5, prior=:none)
mml_pcm = fit(spec_pcm; method=:mmle, quad_points=31)
cmp = compare_models("RSM" => mml_rsm, "PCM" => mml_pcm; criterion=:aic)

save_fit("mml-rsm.jls", mml_rsm)
save_report("mml-rsm.json", rep)

using CairoMakie
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(mml_rsm; kind=:posterior)  # point estimates + 95% asymptotic Wald CIs
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(mml_rsm; kind=:wright)     # multi-facet Wright map
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(mml_rsm; kind=:pathway)    # Infit MNSQ vs. logit Pathway map
BayesianMGMFRM.plot(cmp; kind=:comparison)     # MMLE ΔAIC / ΔBIC comparison

Bayesian-only MCMC operations (posterior_summary, posterior_mcse, waic, psis_loo, prior_sensitivity) and JMLE AIC/BIC comparisons (invalid under Neyman–Scott incidental person parameters) are deterministically refused with guidance to the appropriate frequentist or Bayesian verb.

Can the two groups be compared on this rating scale?

For a scalar MRCML specification, give each modeled person one row in a roster. Choose a reference group and, if needed, numeric covariates. The reference population mean is zero at covariate zero. The fixed category scoring coefficients set the unit. The group coefficient compares means at the same values of all covariates.

ratings = (id=[1,1,2,2,3,3,4,4], item=repeat(["A","B"],4),
           score=[1,0,0,1,1,1,0,0])
spec = mfrm_spec(ratings; person=:id, score=:score, facets=(item=:item,),
    steps=:global, category_levels=0:1,
    fixed_calibration=(facets=(item=Dict("A"=>0.0),),), prior=:none)
roster = (id=[1,2,3,4], study=["first","first","second","second"],
          practice=[0.0,1.0,0.0,1.0])
check = BayesianMGMFRM.MRCML.population_design(spec, roster;
    person=:id, group=:study, reference="first", covariates=(:practice,))
check.mean_terms  # both the second-group contrast and practice slope are estimable

If every person in the second group is rated by a panel with its own free severity shift, increasing the second-group mean and that shift equally leaves every rating probability unchanged. The affected mean gets identifiable=false. Adding a duplicate group indicator as a covariate also flags both affected coefficients. A full rank establishes separation of the declared location terms in the observed design. Group SD precision, measurement invariance and the adequacy of a shared anchor require other evidence.

Estimate the adjusted group difference and group SDs

For your own four-category ratings, supply a long table ratings with id, item and score, and a roster with id, study and practice. In this example both groups see item I1, fixed at −0.8 logits. The study labels include "first". Estimating a separate person SD in each group requires replication beyond the four-person design illustration above.

population = (roster=roster, person=:id, group=:study, reference="first",
              covariates=(:practice,))
measurement = (person=:id, score=:score, facets=(item=:item,), steps=:global,
               category_levels=0:3, fixed_calibration=(facets=(item=Dict("I1"=>-0.8),),))
grouped = mfrm_spec(ratings; measurement..., population, prior=:none)
m = fit(grouped; method=:mmle, quad_points=81)
qualify(m)
design_summary(m).population.coefficients  # adjusted mean contrast and common slope, with SEs
design_summary(m).population.groups        # SD and delta-method SE for each named group

With the same item and step constraints in both groups, a positive contrast means higher expected ability at equal practice. Check covariate overlap before interpreting that comparison. Each person contributes one marginal likelihood across all of their ratings. Standard errors include joint estimation of the free measurement and population coefficients. The SEs above condition on the specified value of item I1.

If the anchors came from an independent sample, transfer their estimates and joint covariance on the same logit scale. For example, suppose a preliminary MMLE fit first_fit names the item difficulties "I1" and "I3" and the first centered step "step-1". Use the actual parameter names from your preliminary fit. Here the receiving data use the same four categories.

mapping = [(parameter="I1", block=:item, level="I1"),
           (parameter="I3", block=:item, level="I3"),
           (parameter="step-1", block=:steps, owner=:global, level=:global, upper_category=1)]
transfer = anchor_linking_summary(first_fit; coordinates=mapping)
linked_groups = mfrm_spec(ratings; person=:id, score=:score, facets=(item=:item,),
    steps=:global, category_levels=0:3, population, calibration=transfer, prior=:none)
m_linked = fit(linked_groups; method=:mmle, quad_points=81)
design_summary(m_linked).calibration.contrasts
item_values = Dict(c.level => c.estimate for c in transfer.coordinates if c.block == :item)
link = anchor_linking_summary(preliminary_ratings, m_linked;
    block=:item, person=:id, reference=item_values, interval=0.95)
link.panel.contrasts

For each group versus the named reference, conditional gives the Wald interval with anchors fixed. summary gives the interval after adding the first-order contribution of their joint sampling covariance. The point estimate is identical. Read the contrast at the same covariate values in both groups. The calculation allows all free receiving coefficients and group SDs to vary with the anchors. SEs reported for covariate slopes and group SDs, and plug-in plausible values, still condition on the supplied calibration. If the receiving information fails qualification, the propagated SE is missing. Independence requires disjoint sampling units across the calibration and receiving stages. The intervals assume stable linking parameters. A systematic change in the meaning or severity of an anchor requires a separate analysis.

For a Bayesian analysis, declare independent Normal priors for the mean coefficients and LogNormal priors for the group SDs. Pass the resulting target to the ordinary sampler. The numerical settings below illustrate invocation. Use the diagnostics and Monte Carlo errors to judge the retained draws.

using BayesianMGMFRM.GlassBox: parameter_names
using Distributions: Normal, LogNormal
target = mfrm_spec(ratings; measurement...,
    prior_sd=(facets=(item=1.,),step=0.75),
    population=merge(population,(coefficient_prior=Normal(0,1.1),sd_prior=LogNormal(0,0.5))))
b = fit(target; method=:nuts, chains=4, warmup=500, ndraws=1000, seed=21721)
qualify(b)
terms = filter(n -> startswith(n,"population"), parameter_names(target;scale=:model))
posterior_summary(b;parameters=terms)
posterior_mcse(b;parameters=terms)
save_fit("grouped.jls", b)
b_saved = load_fit("grouped.jls")

The group means and SDs describe a single latent dimension. The native fit retains the roster and joint draws. For an independent Stan comparison use the target-specific pair BayesianMGMFRM.MRCML.stan_code(target) and stan_data(target).

The Bayesian sampler uses a standardized ability for every person by default (person_coordinates=:noncentered). To sample physical abilities for everyone, add person_coordinates=:centered to the population declaration. To choose individually, supply a dictionary keyed by every original person ID, with either symbol as its value. For example, an existing dictionary by_id can be passed as population=merge(population, priors, (person_coordinates=by_id,)), where priors holds the two population priors above.

To use observed design information, specify person_coordinates=(information_cutoff=8.0,). The sampler centers a person when the sum of the within-rating variances of the category scoring weights, multiplied by the squared prior median group SD, reaches the chosen cutoff. The calculation uses physical ability zero and the measurement parameters at their zero raw prior coordinates. It uses the assigned ratings and their category supports, independently of the scores received. Inspect design_summary(target).population.person_chart in .population.people order. Eight is an optional setting studied on specific rating designs. Estimated calibration, group SDs and category use can change sampling efficiency. Check the whole fit and the Monte Carlo error for the group or person estimate of interest. The fit saves the assignment. Reopen it with a version supporting grouped person coordinates. Use model-scale summaries or joint plausible values to report abilities.

Use plausible values for a population comparison

To compare two groups at the same practice level, start from the grouped fits above. The MMLE route samples a person's ability given their own ratings, group and covariates on the fitted quadrature grid. The measurement coefficients, regression coefficients and group SDs have one fitted value each. The Bayesian route carries all of these quantities and the persons together in each retained draw.

H = BayesianMGMFRM.PortableHandoff
plugin = plausible_values(m; n_imputations=100, seed=7321)
joint = plausible_values(b_saved)
contrast(plugin; group="second", reference="first")
contrast(joint; group="second", reference="first")
first(H.pv_rows(joint))  # imputation, chain, iteration, person, group, theta
receipt = save_report("group-pvs.json", joint; return_receipt=true)
restored = load_report("group-pvs.json"; expected_sha256=receipt.sha256)
contrast(restored; group="second", reference="first")

Use the actual group labels from your roster. For MMLE, each imputation supplies one complete-data group contrast from an unweighted regression with separate group intercepts and common covariate slopes. The within-imputation variance uses the independent-person HC2 sandwich. The result gives the mean contrast, mean within-imputation variance, between-imputation variance, their Rubin total and a large-complete-sample interval. Increase the number of imputations if the reported imputation Monte Carlo error matters at your reporting precision. This interval conditions on the fitted calibration and population parameters. Its repeated-sample coverage depends on the design and imputation model.

The joint result uses the mean and equal-tailed credible interval of the population coefficient difference at shared covariate values. Check qualify(b_saved) and the source diagnostics before interpreting it. Group SDs are available in the fit summary above. In the bundle, record.parameter_names identifies the model-scale columns, including group SDs. Their values are in record.calibration for plug-in PVs and in each record.draws[i].parameters for joint PVs. Tidy person rows identify each imputation and retain original person/group labels. Store the SHA-256 receipt separately for verification.
